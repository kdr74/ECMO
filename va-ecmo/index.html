📖 Overview

Veno-Arterial ECMO (V-A ECMO) is a form of mechanical circulatory support for two main patient groups:

  • Cardiogenic shock with high SCAI grading (SCAI D or E)
  • Refractory cardiac arrest without ROSC – termed "E-CPR"
⚠️ CRITICAL NOTICE

V-A ECMO initiation is a time-sensitive, complex intervention with significant mortality and morbidity risk. Patient selection is vital and should be guided by experienced MDT members.

1️⃣ Section 1: Indications & Patient Selection

📋 Patient Groups for V-A ECMO

V-A ECMO is indicated for two distinct patient populations:

  • Cardiogenic Shock: SCAI D or E shock refractory to conventional therapy
  • Refractory Cardiac Arrest: No ROSC after 3 cycles of ALS (E-CPR)

🫀 V-A ECMO for Cardiogenic Shock

✅ Absolute Requirements (ALL must be met):

  1. SCAI D or E cardiogenic shock (see Appendix 2 for classification)
  2. Persistent shock despite:
    • Escalating inotropes/vasopressors (≥2 agents)
    • Mechanical ventilation optimised
    • Fluid resuscitation appropriate
    • Reversible causes addressed
  3. Suitable candidate as assessed by:
    • Bristol ECMO consultant
    • RBH/HH advanced heart failure team

🎯 Patient Suitability Assessment

Use the risk stratification matrix below to guide decision-making. Patients are stratified by:

  • Age: <50, 50-60, 60-70, >70 years
  • Frailty/Comorbidity burden: Low, Moderate, High
  • Cardiac diagnosis: Acute MI, decompensated heart failure, other
  • Neurological status: GCS, response to pain, pupil reactivity

Figure 1: V-A ECMO risk stratification matrix for cardiogenic shock. Green = proceed, Amber = MDT discussion required, Red = high risk (consultant discussion), Blue = inappropriate

🛑 DO NOT PROCEED INDEPENDENTLY IF:
  • Patient in RED zone (High Risk) - requires senior consultant discussion with RBH/HH
  • Patient in BLUE zone (Inappropriate) - ECMO not indicated
  • SAVE Score Class III-V - predicted in-hospital mortality >75%
  • Unwitnessed collapse with unknown downtime
  • Advanced frailty (Clinical Frailty Scale ≥7)

Action: For RED/BLUE zone patients, escalate immediately to Bristol ECMO consultant who will liaise with RBH/HH. Do NOT delay stabilisation while awaiting decision.

💔 V-A ECMO for Refractory Cardiac Arrest (E-CPR)

✅ Inclusion Criteria:

  • Witnessed cardiac arrest (in-hospital or OHCA with bystander CPR)
  • Shockable rhythm (VF/pVT) or suspected cardiac cause
  • Age 18-70 years (consider up to 75 if excellent baseline function)
  • No ROSC after 3 cycles of ALS (approximately 15 minutes)
  • CPR quality: End-tidal CO₂ >10 mmHg (suggests viable perfusion)
  • Reversible cause likely (e.g. acute MI, PE, toxicology)

⏱️ Time Windows:

Arrest Location Low Flow Time Action
In-hospital <60 minutes Consider E-CPR
OHCA (bystander CPR) <60 minutes total Consider E-CPR if reversible cause
Any >60 minutes E-CPR unlikely beneficial

🛑 STOP CRITERIA FOR E-CPR

Do NOT proceed if ANY of the following apply:

Clinical STOP Criteria (ANY = STOP)

  • ⛔ Unwitnessed arrest with unknown downtime
  • ⛔ No bystander CPR for OHCA
  • ⛔ Non-shockable rhythm throughout (unless clear reversible cause)
  • ⛔ Known DNAR/ceiling of treatment in place
  • ⛔ Terminal illness or severe frailty (CFS ≥7)
  • ⛔ Major trauma or active bleeding
  • ⛔ Known severe aortic regurgitation (moderate-severe AR)

Physiological STOP Criteria (≥2 = STOP)

  • 📉 pH <7.0 despite high-quality CPR
  • 📉 Lactate >15 mmol/L
  • 📉 End-tidal CO₂ <10 mmHg consistently
  • 📉 Pupils fixed and dilated bilaterally
  • 📉 No respiratory effort despite attempts to ventilate
⚠️ E-CPR Activation Process:
  1. 3 cycles ALS completed (approx. 15 min) without ROSC
  2. Team leader assesses STOP criteria
  3. Activate E-CPR pathway: Call ICU Outreach Registrar (Ext 27139) or Switchboard (2222) for ECMO consultant
  4. Continue high-quality CPR while team assembles
  5. Target cannulation within 20 minutes of E-CPR activation

See Appendix 3 for detailed E-CPR algorithm

🚫 Absolute Contraindications to V-A ECMO

  • Advanced directives: DNAR, ceiling of treatment limiting escalation
  • Severe aortic regurgitation: Moderate-severe AR (will worsen on V-A ECMO)
  • Aortic dissection: Unless surgical repair planned concurrently
  • Uncontrolled bleeding: Active haemorrhage not amenable to control
  • Severe peripheral vascular disease: Inability to cannulate femoral vessels
  • Known irreversible brain injury: Established anoxic brain injury, massive CVA
  • Terminal malignancy: Life expectancy <1 year from cancer
  • Severe chronic organ dysfunction: End-stage renal, liver, or lung disease not amenable to transplant

⚠️ Relative Contraindications (Require Senior Discussion)

  • Age >70 years: Assess frailty, comorbidities, baseline function
  • BMI >40 kg/m²: Technical challenges, higher complication rates
  • Recent major surgery: Within 7 days (bleeding risk)
  • Chronic renal failure: Requiring dialysis (unless transplant candidate)
  • Known CNS disease: Previous stroke, dementia affecting baseline function
  • Immunosuppression: Active chemotherapy, organ transplant
  • Severe frailty: Clinical Frailty Scale 6-7
  • Prolonged low-flow time: >45-60 minutes CPR (neurological outcome uncertain)

Action: Discuss all relative contraindications with Bristol ECMO consultant before proceeding. Document rationale clearly.

2️⃣ Section 2: Location & Facility Requirements

🏥 Preferred Cannulation Locations

Optimal Sites (in order of preference):

  1. Cardiac Catheterisation Laboratory
    • ✅ Fluoroscopy available for cannula positioning
    • ✅ Sterile environment
    • ✅ Familiar to interventional cardiologists
    • ✅ Equipment readily available
    • ❌ May have scheduling conflicts
  2. Cardiac Operating Theatre
    • ✅ Optimal for post-cardiotomy cases
    • ✅ Full surgical support immediately available
    • ✅ Fluoroscopy usually available
    • ✅ Best for difficult access / revision surgery
    • ❌ Requires theatre availability
  3. Critical Care Unit (A600 / C604)
    • ✅ Patient already in situ (E-CPR, deteriorating patient)
    • ✅ Familiar environment for ICU team
    • ✅ Immediate availability
    • ❌ No fluoroscopy (rely on echo/CXR for position confirmation)
    • ❌ Less optimal infection control
    • ⚠️ Acceptable if patient too unstable to transfer
Decision Framework:
  • Elective/Semi-elective: Cath lab or theatre (patient stable for transfer)
  • Emergency/E-CPR: Bedside in ICU (patient cannot be moved safely)
  • Post-cardiotomy: Cardiac theatre (surgical support essential)
  • Deteriorating on ICU: Assess stability - transfer if safe, otherwise bedside

  • Active bleeding requiring surgical intervention or high re-exploration risk
  • Chest open (delayed sternal closure)
  • Recent cardiac surgery requiring close cardiothoracic monitoring
  • Need for immediate theatre access (<30 minutes)
  • Central cannulation via sternotomy

A600 (General ICU) - Typical Patients:

  • Medical cardiogenic shock (AMI, myocarditis, decompensated heart failure)
  • E-CPR survivors
  • Post-cardiotomy patients after 48-72hrs if:
    • Bleeding controlled (<50ml/hr chest drain output)
    • Haemodynamically stable
    • Chest closed
    • No anticipated return to theatre
  • Peripheral (femoral) cannulation patients
  • Long-term ECMO support anticipated
⚠️ Transfer Between Units:

ECMO patients CAN be transferred between units if clinically indicated, but requires:

  • Senior consultant decision: ECMO consultant approval mandatory
  • Full handover: Comprehensive handover from sending to receiving consultant
  • Perfusionist accompanies: Never transfer ECMO patient without perfusionist
  • Emergency equipment:
    • Hand-crank for pump failure
    • Clamps for circuit disconnection
    • Emergency drugs (vasopressors, atropine, adrenaline)
    • Airway equipment and defibrillator
  • Receiving unit prepared: Bed space ready, receiving team briefed
  • Transport within hospital: Use dedicated bed (ECMO circuit secured), extra staff

Never transfer ECMO patient unaccompanied or without full preparation

🚁 Transfer to External Sites (RBH/HH)

When Transfer is Indicated:

  • Failed weaning trial but suitable for advanced therapies (LVAD, transplant assessment)
  • Complex cases requiring specialist input (e.g., structural heart disease, complex congenital)
  • Day 5-7 decision point: not weaning, still suitable, requires long-term plan

Transfer Logistics:

Pre-Transfer Preparation (24-48 hours before):

  1. RBH/HH acceptance confirmed: Formal acceptance by receiving consultant
  2. ENS coordinator notified: 07741123467 - coordinates transfer logistics
  3. Retrieval team briefed: RBH/HH ECMO retrieval team (usually 2 perfusionists + doctor)
  4. Patient optimized:
    • ECMO flows stable
    • No active bleeding
    • Haemodynamically stable
    • Adequate sedation/analgesia
  5. Equipment prepared: Portable ECMO circuit, monitoring equipment, drugs

Transfer Method:

  • Road ambulance: Standard (Bristol → London ~3 hours)
    • Specialist retrieval ambulance with ECMO circuit secured
    • Retrieval team accompanies patient
    • Bristol perfusionist may travel to handover
  • Helicopter: Rare (only if time-critical and weather permits)
    • Limited use due to space constraints with ECMO circuit
    • Usually reserved for absolute emergencies

Documentation for Transfer:

  • Complete ECMO flow sheet (all settings, complications, interventions)
  • Comprehensive discharge summary
  • All imaging (echo reports, CXRs, CTs on disc)
  • Medication kardex and drug chart
  • Microbiology results and antibiotic history
  • Family contact details and next of kin information

📝 Step-by-Step Cannulation

Step 1: Femoral Vein Cannulation

  1. Ultrasound-guided puncture of common femoral vein
  2. Insert guidewire under US/fluoroscopic guidance
  3. Confirm wire in IVC/RA with echo (TOE) or fluoroscopy
  4. Serial dilation over wire (dilators 8Fr → 14Fr → 18Fr → 22Fr)
  5. Insert 21-25Fr multistage venous cannula
  6. Target depth: 55cm at skin (tip in RA or SVC-RA junction)
  7. Confirm position with TOE - should see all side holes in RA/SVC
  8. Secure with 2-0 silk purse-string suture
  9. Connect to venous limb of circuit (blue line)

Step 2: Femoral Artery Cannulation

  1. Palpate or US-guided puncture of common femoral artery
  2. Insert guidewire - confirm in descending aorta
  3. Serial dilation (8Fr → 14Fr → 16Fr)
  4. Insert 15-17Fr single-stage arterial cannula
  5. Target depth: 15-20cm (tip in descending thoracic aorta)
  6. Confirm position with fluoroscopy or CXR
  7. Secure with 2-0 silk purse-string suture
  8. Do NOT connect to circuit yet - DPC must be inserted first
⚠️ CRITICAL: DPC Insertion BEFORE ECMO Flow

Distal perfusion cannula MUST be inserted and confirmed working before establishing ECMO flow. Failure to do so results in acute limb ischaemia within minutes.

Step 3: Distal Perfusion Cannula (DPC) Insertion

  1. Puncture superficial femoral artery (SFA) distal to arterial cannula
  2. Insert guidewire antegrade into popliteal artery
  3. Insert 6-9Fr armoured DPC (15-20cm length)
  4. Confirm placement: distal pulses return, doppler signals in foot
  5. Secure with 3-0 silk suture
  6. Leave DPC clamped until connection to ECMO circuit

Step 4: DPC Connection to Arterial Cannula (Critical Procedure)

⚠️ Patient will be OFF ECMO during this step - must be completed rapidly

Figure 4a: SHORT male-to-male Luer connector (CRITICAL - long connectors create dead space and air traps)

Figure 4b: Initial layout - arterial cannula side port (green), DPC, SHORT Luer connector, extension tubing

  1. Prepare equipment:
    • SHORT male-to-male Luer connector (2-3cm max)
    • 50ml syringe for aspirating air
    • Extension tubing (pre-filled with heparinised saline)
    • Two clamps ready
  2. Inform team: "Pausing ECMO for DPC connection - 2 minute procedure"
  3. Clamp arterial line proximally (between patient and circuit)
  4. Pause ECMO pump
  5. Position clamps either side of arterial cannula side port

Figure 4c: Clamps positioned - isolates side port from main lumen

  1. Connect DPC: Attach DPC to side port via SHORT Luer connector
  2. De-air the system: Use 50ml syringe to aspirate from DPC until blood-filled

Figure 4d: Aspirating with 50ml syringe - critical to remove all air before opening clamps

  1. Remove distal clamp (between side port and DPC)
  2. Remove proximal clamp (between side port and arterial cannula tip)
  3. Restart ECMO flow
  4. Confirm DPC patency: Should see pulsatile flow, warm foot, doppler signals

Figure 4e: Final configuration - DPC receiving pulsatile flow from arterial side port

⚠️ Common Errors to Avoid:
  • ❌ Using LONG male-to-male connectors (creates air traps)
  • ❌ Inadequate de-airing before opening clamps (air embolism to foot)
  • ❌ Connecting DPC AFTER starting ECMO (limb ischaemia)
  • ❌ Kinking DPC during positioning (loss of distal perfusion)

Step 5: Initiate ECMO Flow

  1. Perfusionist confirms: Circuit primed, de-aired, all connections secure
  2. Team brief: "Ready to start flows - confirm haemodynamics stable"
  3. Start pump: Begin at 1.0 L/min
  4. Incrementally increase: 1.0 → 2.0 → 3.0 → target flow
  5. Target flow: 60-70ml/kg/min (usually 3.5-4.5 L/min for 70kg adult)
  6. Monitor continuously:
    • Arterial line waveform (should maintain pulsatility)
    • Venous line pressure (should be slightly negative)
    • Circuit flows and pressures
    • Patient MAP, lactate, SvO₂
  7. Commence anticoagulation: See Section 4 for protocol

✅ Post-Cannulation Confirmation

Immediate (within 5 minutes):

  • ☐ Bilateral limb perfusion: Warm, capillary refill <2s, doppler signals present
  • ☐ Adequate ECMO flows: 3-4 L/min minimum (60ml/kg/min)
  • ☐ Haemodynamics improving: MAP 60-70, lactate trending down
  • ☐ SvO₂ >60%: Confirms adequate flow
  • ☐ Post-oxy blood gas PaO₂ >200 mmHg: Confirms oxygenator function

Within 1 hour:

  • ☐ TOE: Confirm venous cannula position, assess LV loading
  • ☐ CXR: Confirm arterial cannula tip in descending aorta (T6-T8 level)
  • ☐ ABG: Improving acidosis, lactate clearance
  • ☐ Cerebral NIRS applied: Bilateral frontal monitoring
  • ☐ PA catheter inserted (if not already in situ) - essential for LV monitoring
  • ☐ Documentation complete: Procedure note, complication record

🫀 LV Loading Strategy (MOST CRITICAL)

Figure 5: LV unloading physiological cascade - targets and interventions

Goal: Minimize LV afterload whilst maintaining systemic perfusion. LV distension is the MOST COMMON complication (30% of cases) and MUST be prevented.

Detection of LV Distension:

  • 📉 Pulse pressure <10 mmHg (aortic valve not opening)
  • 📉 LVOT VTI <5 cm on echo
  • 📉 PADP/PCWP >18 mmHg and rising
  • 📉 No aortic valve opening on TOE/TTE
  • 📉 Dilated, akinetic LV on echo
  • 📉 New arrhythmias (VT/VF from subendocardial ischaemia)

Prevention Strategy:

Step 1: Optimize ECMO Parameters

  • Use MINIMUM ECMO flows that maintain perfusion (often 2-3 L/min is adequate)
  • Target MAP 50-55 mmHg initially (avoid excessive afterload)
  • Accept lower MAP if end-organ perfusion adequate (lactate clearing, urine output maintained)

Step 2: Maintain Native Cardiac Output

  • Inotropes: Continue or start low-dose dobutamine (2.5-5 mcg/kg/min)
  • Afterload reduction: Consider milrinone 0.375-0.5 mcg/kg/min
  • Rhythm control: Maintain sinus rhythm if possible (cardiovert VT/VF)
  • Avoid excessive vasopressors: Minimize noradrenaline to lowest effective dose

Step 3: Echo Monitoring

  • TTE/TOE every 6-12 hours minimum
  • Assess: LV size, wall motion, aortic valve opening, LVOT VTI
  • Red flags: Dilating LV, reducing ejection, static blood in LV apex
⚠️ If LV Distension Develops Despite Above:

Proceed to LV Unloading Device - see Section 5 for detailed protocol

Options (in order of preference):

  1. IABP 1:1 - first line (40ml balloon)
  2. Impella CP - if IABP inadequate (P6-P9 support)
  3. Surgical LV vent - refractory cases

💉 Anticoagulation Protocol

Timing of Initiation:

Clinical Scenario Start Heparin Rationale
Standard V-A ECMO Immediately post-cannulation Standard practice
Post-cardiotomy Delay 4-6 hours if possible High bleeding risk
Active bleeding Delay until haemostasis Bleeding > thrombosis risk acutely
Recent neurosurgery/ICH MDT discussion - may avoid entirely Catastrophic bleeding risk

Unfractionated Heparin Protocol:

Loading Dose

  • Standard: 50-75 units/kg IV bolus
  • High bleeding risk: 25-50 units/kg or omit bolus
  • Post-cardiotomy: NO bolus - start infusion only

Maintenance Infusion

  • Start: 10-15 units/kg/hr (approx. 1000 units/hr for 70kg)
  • Target Anti-Xa: 0.3-0.5 IU/ml
  • Check Anti-Xa: 4 hours after starting, then every 6 hours until stable
  • Once stable: Daily Anti-Xa monitoring

Anti-Xa Adjustments:

5️⃣ Section 5: Complications & Emergency Management

⚠️ Common V-A ECMO Complications

Complications occur in 40-60% of V-A ECMO cases. Early recognition and prompt management are essential.

  • Most common: LV distension (30%), bleeding (30%), limb ischaemia (10-25%)
  • Life-threatening: Harlequin syndrome, massive bleeding, circuit failure, cardiac tamponade
  • Daily monitoring essential for all complication red flags

🫀 LV Distension Syndrome (30% of cases)

Pathophysiology:

V-A ECMO increases LV afterload by:

  • Retrograde aortic flow increases diastolic pressure
  • Impaired LV ejection → increased end-diastolic volume
  • Rising LV end-diastolic pressure → pulmonary oedema
  • Subendocardial ischaemia → further contractility reduction
  • Vicious cycle → LV dilatation, stasis, thrombus formation

⚠️ Detection (ANY of these = HIGH CONCERN):

Clinical Sign Threshold Severity
Pulse pressure ↓ <10-15 mmHg Moderate concern
No aortic valve opening Confirmed on echo Severe - urgent action
LVOT VTI <5 cm Severe - urgent action
PADP / PCWP ↑ >18 mmHg and rising Moderate-severe
Dilated, akinetic LV Progressive on serial echo Severe - urgent action
Pulmonary oedema Worsening on CXR/SpO₂ Moderate-severe
Ventricular arrhythmias New onset VT/VF Severe - urgent action

🚨 Management Algorithm:

STEP 1: Optimize ECMO & Medical Therapy (IMMEDIATE)

  1. ↓ ECMO flows to MINIMUM tolerated
    • Reduce to 2.0-2.5 L/min (monitor lactate, SvO₂, end-organs)
    • Goal: Reduce LV afterload whilst maintaining perfusion
  2. ↓ MAP to 50-55 mmHg
    • Wean vasopressors aggressively
    • Accept lower MAP if perfusion adequate
  3. ↑ Inotropic support
    • Dobutamine: Increase to 5-10 mcg/kg/min
    • Milrinone: Consider 0.5 mcg/kg/min (inodilator - reduces afterload)
    • Goal: Improve LV contractility to restore ejection
  4. Optimize preload
    • Diuresis: Furosemide 40-80mg IV bolus, then infusion
    • Target CVP 8-10 mmHg (avoid hypervolaemia)
  5. Rhythm control
    • Maintain sinus rhythm (cardiovert VT/VF)
    • Amiodarone if recurrent arrhythmias

Reassess with echo after 1-2 hours

STEP 2: LV Venting Device (if Step 1 fails)

Indications for mechanical unloading:

  • No improvement in LV distension after 1-2 hours of Step 1
  • Persistent PADP/PCWP >18 mmHg
  • Worsening pulmonary oedema despite diuresis
  • Recurrent VT/VF despite cardioversion
  • Progressive LV dilatation on echo

Option 1: IABP (First-Line Mechanical Unloading)

  • Balloon size: 40ml (standard adult)
  • Trigger: 1:1 ratio with R wave
  • Insertion: Contralateral femoral artery (opposite to ECMO arterial cannula)
  • Position: Tip 2cm below L subclavian (confirm CXR/fluoro)
  • Augmentation: 300ml (set volume to achieve this)
  • Mechanism: Diastolic augmentation ↑ coronary perfusion, systolic deflation ↓ LV afterload
  • Success rate: 60-70% achieve adequate LV unloading
IABP + V-A ECMO Monitoring:
  • Echo every 6 hours - assess LV size, AV opening, LVOT VTI
  • Target: Pulse pressure >15 mmHg, AV opening each beat, LVOT VTI >10cm
  • PADP should decrease to <18 mmHg within 6-12 hours
  • Pulmonary oedema should improve on CXR

Option 2: Impella CP (if IABP Inadequate)

  • Indications: Inadequate response to IABP after 12-24 hours
  • Device: Impella CP (3.7L/min flow) or Impella 5.5 (6L/min - surgical)
  • Setting: P6-P9 (maximum unloading)
  • Insertion: Axillary artery cut-down (preferred) or femoral (alternate groin)
  • Position: Across AV into LV, motor in ascending aorta
  • Mechanism: Active LV decompression - aspirates blood from LV → aorta
  • Success rate: 80-90% achieve LV unloading
  • Complications: Haemolysis (monitor LDH, plasma-free Hb), device malposition

Option 3: Surgical LV Vent (Refractory Cases)

  • Indications: Failed IABP + Impella, refractory LV distension
  • Technique: Apical vent via left thoracotomy or sternotomy
  • Drainage: LV apex → venous reservoir of ECMO circuit
  • Risk: Requires surgical procedure, bleeding risk, arrhythmias
  • Success: Very effective but highest morbidity
🚨 When to Escalate:

Contact Bristol ECMO Consultant + Cardiothoracic Surgery + RBH/HH if:

  • LV distension not improving with Step 1 after 2 hours
  • Recurrent VT/VF despite optimal medical therapy
  • Worsening pulmonary oedema requiring ↑ FiO₂ >0.6
  • Need for mechanical LV unloading device

🎭 Harlequin Syndrome (North-South Syndrome)

Figure 6: Harlequin syndrome - differential hypoxaemia between upper (cyanotic) and lower (pink) body due to poor native lung function and mixing point in aorta

Pathophysiology:

Occurs when native heart ejects poorly-oxygenated blood that "competes" with well-oxygenated ECMO blood:

  • Native LV ejects hypoxaemic blood (due to lung failure) into proximal aorta
  • ECMO returns oxygenated blood to femoral artery (retrograde flow)
  • Mixing point typically at aortic arch
  • Upper body (brain, coronaries) receives hypoxaemic native cardiac output
  • Lower body receives well-oxygenated ECMO blood
  • Results in differential cyanosis - blue upper body, pink lower body

⚠️ Detection:

Measurement Threshold for Concern Measurement Site
Right arm SpO₂ <85-90% Pre-ductal (right hand pulse ox)
Right radial ABG PaO₂ <8 kPa (60 mmHg) Right radial arterial line
Cerebral NIRS <55% or ↓>10% from baseline Bilateral frontal lobes
Post-oxy PaO₂ >200 mmHg (GOOD) Confirms ECMO oxygenator working
Lower limb SpO₂ >95% (GOOD) Confirms ECMO providing O₂ to lower body

Clinical Features:

  • Visible cyanosis of head, neck, upper limbs
  • Pink/normal colour of abdomen, lower limbs
  • Altered consciousness (cerebral hypoxaemia)
  • Myocardial ischaemia (ECG changes, troponin rise)
  • Metabolic acidosis despite adequate ECMO flows

🚨 Management Algorithm:

STEP 1: Optimize Native Lung Oxygenation (IMMEDIATE)

  1. ↑ FiO₂ to 1.0 (ventilator)
  2. ↑ PEEP
    • Increase stepwise to 12-15 cmH₂O
    • Perform recruitment manoeuvres (sustained inflation 30-40 cmH₂O × 30 seconds)
    • Monitor response: repeat right radial ABG after 15 minutes
  3. Optimize ventilation
    • Ensure adequate tidal volumes (6-8 ml/kg IBW)
    • Check for pneumothorax (urgent CXR if suspected)
    • Suction ETT (exclude mucus plug)
    • Consider bronchoscopy if lobar collapse suspected
  4. Prone positioning (if severe ARDS and refractory hypoxaemia)

STEP 2: Optimize ECMO Parameters

  1. ↑ ECMO flow to maximum tolerated
    • Increase to 4.5-6 L/min (if patient tolerates)
    • Goal: Push mixing point more proximally (towards aortic arch)
    • Monitor for LV distension (may worsen with high flows)
  2. ↑ FdO₂ (sweep gas) to 1.0
    • Maximize oxygen delivery via ECMO
    • Helps but does NOT solve differential hypoxaemia

STEP 3: Advanced Interventions (if Steps 1-2 fail)

Option A: Upper Body Arterial Cannulation

  • Indication: Persistent cerebral/myocardial hypoxaemia despite Steps 1-2
  • Sites:
    • Right axillary/subclavian artery (preferred - closest to head)
    • Right carotid artery (high risk - stroke)
    • Ascending aorta (surgical - open chest)
  • Technique: 8-10Fr arterial cannula, delivers oxygenated ECMO blood to upper body
  • Configuration: Y-connector from ECMO arterial limb → femoral + upper body cannulas
  • Flows: Split flow (e.g., 3L to femoral + 2L to axillary)
  • Risk: Upper limb ischaemia, brachial plexus injury, surgical access required

Option B: VV-VA Hybrid Configuration

  • Indication: Isolated lung failure (heart recovering), persistent Harlequin
  • Technique: Convert femoral arterial cannula → venous drainage + add IJV venous return
  • Configuration: VV ECMO (supports lungs) + reduced VA support
  • Benefit: Oxygenated blood goes through native heart → natural distribution
  • Risk: Requires cardiac recovery sufficient for VV support

Option C: Full VV Conversion

  • Indication: Cardiac function recovered, isolated respiratory failure
  • Technique: Remove arterial cannula, convert to VV ECMO (dual-lumen or two-site)
  • Benefit: Eliminates differential hypoxaemia, reduces LV afterload
  • Requirement: Adequate cardiac function (LVEF >30%, no vasopressors)
🚨 Immediate Escalation Required If:
  • Right radial PaO₂ <6 kPa (<45 mmHg) despite Step 1
  • Cerebral NIRS <50% or rapidly declining
  • Myocardial ischaemia (ST changes, rising troponin, arrhythmias)
  • Deteriorating consciousness (GCS dropping)

Action: Contact Bristol ECMO Consultant + RBH/HH for upper body cannulation vs VV conversion

🦵 Limb Ischaemia (10-25% of cases)

Risk Factors:

  • Large arterial cannula (17Fr+) relative to vessel size
  • Peripheral vascular disease
  • Female sex (smaller vessels)
  • DPC malfunction or kinking
  • Vasopressor use (peripheral vasoconstriction)
  • Hypovolaemia

⚠️ Detection (Check Every 2 Hours):

Assessment Normal Ischaemia
Skin colour Pink, warm Mottled, pale, dusky
Temperature Warm to touch Cool or cold
Capillary refill <2 seconds >3 seconds
Doppler signals Biphasic in DP/PT Absent or monophasic
Motor function Can flex toes Weakness or paralysis
Pain None Severe pain (if conscious)

Classification of Severity:

  • Stage I (Threatened): Cool, reduced pulses, salvageable with intervention
  • Stage IIa (Marginal): Cool, absent pulses, sensory loss, requires urgent intervention
  • Stage IIb (Immediate): Cool, motor deficit, immediate intervention needed
  • Stage III (Irreversible): Compartment syndrome, muscle necrosis, amputation likely

🚨 Management:

First-Line Measures (Immediate):

  1. Check DPC patency
    • Ensure no kinking of DPC tubing
    • Flush DPC with heparinised saline (assess resistance)
    • Verify pulsatile flow in DPC (should see "bounce" with pulse)
  2. Optimize systemic perfusion
    • Ensure adequate ECMO flows (4-5 L/min)
    • MAP 65-70 mmHg (increase if needed)
    • Correct hypovolaemia
  3. Reduce vasopressors if possible (causes peripheral vasoconstriction)
  4. Ensure adequate anticoagulation (Anti-Xa 0.3-0.5)

Second-Line: DPC Revision (if no improvement in 30-60 minutes)

  • Indication: Persistent ischaemia despite optimized DPC flow
  • Technique:
    • Re-position DPC (may be too proximal or malpositioned)
    • Upsize DPC if flow inadequate (6Fr → 8Fr)
    • Insert additional DPC to popliteal artery (if SFA occluded)
  • Vascular surgery consult if complex revision needed

Third-Line: Fasciotomy (if compartment syndrome develops)

  • Indications:
    • Compartment pressure >30 mmHg (or >20 mmHg with poor perfusion)
    • Tense, swollen compartments
    • Progressive motor/sensory deficit
  • Urgent vascular surgery - 4-compartment fasciotomy of lower leg
  • Timing critical: Must be performed within 6 hours of onset to prevent permanent damage
🚨 Urgent Vascular Surgery Required If:
  • Stage IIb ischaemia (motor deficit)
  • No improvement after DPC revision
  • Compartment syndrome suspected
  • Threatened limb viability

Do NOT delay: Irreversible damage occurs within 6-8 hours

🩸 Bleeding Complications (30% of cases)

Common Sites:

  • Cannulation sites (most common)
  • Surgical sites (post-cardiotomy, post-sternotomy)
  • GI tract (stress ulceration)
  • Retroperitoneal (femoral vessel injury)
  • Intracranial (catastrophic - 2-8% of cases)

Risk Factors:

  • Anticoagulation (essential but increases bleeding)
  • Thrombocytopaenia (consumption, dilution, heparin-induced)
  • Acquired coagulopathy (factor consumption)
  • Platelet dysfunction (shear stress in circuit)
  • Post-cardiotomy (highest risk)

Management Strategy:

Minor Bleeding (cannulation site ooze, small haematoma):

  • Local pressure (sandbag, manual compression)
  • Reduce heparin by 20-30% (accept Anti-Xa 0.2-0.3)
  • Ensure platelets >80 × 10⁹/L
  • Tranexamic acid 1g IV (if not contraindicated)

Major Bleeding (requiring transfusion, haemodynamic instability):

  1. STOP heparin infusion
  2. Activate massive transfusion protocol
    • Target Hb >80 g/L (consider >100 if myocardial ischaemia)
    • Platelets >100 × 10⁹/L
    • Fibrinogen >1.5 g/L (give cryoprecipitate if low)
    • PT/APTT <1.5× normal (FFP if prolonged)
  3. Consider protamine if life-threatening bleeding (0.5-1mg per 100 units heparin given in last 4 hours)
  4. Tranexamic acid 1g IV, then 1g over 8 hours
  5. Source control:
    • Surgical exploration if bleeding from cannulation site/chest
    • Endoscopy if GI bleed suspected
    • CT if retroperitoneal bleed suspected

Post-Cardiotomy Bleeding (Special Considerations):

Chest Drain Output Action
<100 ml/hr Monitor, ensure adequate anticoagulation
100-200 ml/hr × 2-4 hours Hold heparin, correct coagulopathy, consider re-exploration
>200 ml/hr × 2 hours Urgent re-exploration (high risk of tamponade)
>400 ml/hr × 1 hour Immediate re-exploration
🚨 Intracranial Haemorrhage:

Suspect if: deteriorating GCS, new focal neurology, unequal pupils, seizures

  • STOP heparin immediately
  • Urgent CT head
  • Reverse anticoagulation: Protamine 1mg per 100 units heparin
  • Neurosurgery consult urgently
  • Consider stopping ECMO if catastrophic bleed and poor prognosis

Circuit-Related:

Infectious:

  • Cannula site infection: Daily inspection, swabs if erythema/purulence, antibiotics, consider cannula change
  • Bloodstream infection: Blood cultures from circuit + patient, broad-spectrum antibiotics, line removal if indicated
  • VAP: Common due to prolonged ventilation - targeted antibiotics, daily sedation breaks, wean vent when able

Neurological:

  • Hypoxic brain injury: From pre-ECMO arrest or Harlequin - daily neuro assessment, avoid sedation holidays first 48hrs
  • Stroke (ischaemic): 2-4% incidence - CT head if suspected, anticoagulation challenging, neurology consult
  • Seizures: Continuous EEG if concern, treat with levetiracetam (dose adjust for renal function)

Renal:

  • AKI: Common (60-70%) - optimize ECMO flows, avoid nephrotoxins, CVVHDF if oliguria/fluid overload
  • Haemolysis: From circuit shear stress - monitor LDH, plasma-free Hb, check for circuit issues

🎯 Weaning Trial Protocol

Preparation:

  1. Optimize patient condition:
    • Euvolaemic (CVP 8-12 mmHg)
    • Haemoglobin 80-100 g/L
    • Electrolytes corrected (K⁺ >4.0, Mg²⁺ >1.0)
    • Temperature normothermic
  2. Full echo assessment: TTE or TOE documenting baseline cardiac function
  3. Baseline monitoring in place: Art line, PA catheter, NIRS, continuous ECG
  4. Senior team present: ECMO consultant, intensivist, perfusionist

Weaning Trial Steps:

Phase 1: Baseline Assessment (ECMO Running Full)

  • ECMO flows: Full support (4-5 L/min)
  • Obtain: ABG, lactate, SvO₂, echo
  • Record: HR, BP, CVP, PADP/PCWP, cardiac index

Phase 2: Gradual Flow Reduction

  1. Reduce flows to 3.0 L/min → Observe 10 minutes
    • Monitor: MAP, HR, rhythm, NIRS
    • If stable → proceed
  2. Reduce flows to 2.0 L/min → Observe 10 minutes
    • Repeat monitoring
    • If stable → proceed
  3. Reduce flows to 1.5 L/min → KEY ASSESSMENT POINT
    • Maintain at 1.5 L/min for 30 minutes
    • Perform comprehensive assessment (see success criteria below)

Phase 3: Formal Assessment at 1.5 L/min × 30 minutes

Clinical Assessment:

  • Patient comfortable, no distress
  • No arrhythmias
  • Adequate perfusion (warm peripheries, CRT <2s)
  • Urine output maintained (>0.5 ml/kg/hr)

Haemodynamic Targets:

✅ PASS Criteria (ALL must be met):
  • All haemodynamic targets achieved
  • All echo targets achieved
  • Lactate not rising
  • No arrhythmias during trial
  • Patient clinically stable

❌ FAIL Criteria (ANY of these = failure):
  • MAP <60 mmHg or requiring escalating vasopressors
  • PADP/PCWP rising >20 mmHg
  • Cardiac index <2.0 L/min/m²
  • Rising lactate (>0.5 mmol/L increase)
  • SvO₂ <55%
  • New arrhythmias (VT/VF/AF)
  • Cerebral NIRS dropping <50%
  • Echo: dilating LV, worsening MR/TR, LVOT VTI <8cm

→ Abort trial, return to full flows, reassess in 24-48 hours

🔧 Decannulation Procedure

⚠️ Location & Team Requirements
  • Location: Cardiac theatres (preferred) or cath lab with surgical backup
  • Team: ECMO consultant, cardiac surgeon, anaesthetist, perfusionist, theatre team
  • Timing: Within 12-24 hours of successful wean trial (avoid delay - thrombosis risk)

Pre-Decannulation Preparation:

  • Stop heparin 2-4 hours prior (or give protamine if urgent)
  • Check coagulation: INR <1.5, platelets >80, fibrinogen >1.5
  • Crossmatch: 4 units RBC available
  • Equipment ready: Vascular surgery set, large sutures, haemostatic agents
  • Monitoring: Art line, PA catheter, TOE probe ready

Decannulation Steps:

  1. Final echo: Confirm cardiac function stable
  2. Stop ECMO: Flows to zero, clamp lines
  3. Remove venous cannula: Manual pressure × 15-20 minutes
  4. Remove arterial cannula: Vessel repair (6-0 Prolene running suture or surgical closure)
  5. Remove DPC: Manual pressure
  6. Achieve haemostasis: May require surgical exploration if bleeding persists
  7. Wound closure: Layered closure, drains if significant haematoma
  8. Post-procedure echo: Within 1 hour - ensure cardiac function maintained

Post-Decannulation Care (First 24 Hours):

  • Remain intubated initially (wean ventilation as able)
  • Continue PA catheter monitoring (remove when stable)
  • Echo at 6 and 24 hours post-decannulation
  • Monitor for bleeding (groin inspection every 2 hours)
  • Resume anticoagulation when haemostasis secure (usually 12-24hrs)
  • Restart cardiac medications (ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker when stable)

🎯 Destination Planning by Day 5-7

Every V-A ECMO patient requires clear destination planning by days 5-7:

Outcome 1: Successful Wean (Best Case)

  • Criteria: Passes weaning trial, decannulation successful
  • Pathway:
    • Decannulation in cardiac theatres
    • Remain A600 (ICU) until medically stable
    • Step-down to CCU/ward when appropriate
    • Cardiac rehabilitation
    • Outpatient follow-up with advanced heart failure team
  • Prognosis: Good - most survive to discharge with appropriate therapy

Outcome 2: Failed Wean but Suitable for Advanced Therapies

  • Criteria: Fails weaning trial BUT good functional status, no contraindications
  • Assessment:
    • Age <70 (ideally <65 for transplant)
    • No severe frailty (CFS <5)
    • No active malignancy
    • Adequate end-organ function
    • Good neurological outcome (GCS 15 or improving)
    • Psychosocial support in place
  • Pathway:
    • Transfer to RBH/HH for advanced therapies
    • Options: durable LVAD, heart transplant assessment
    • Coordinate via Bristol ECMO consultant + RBH/HH team
    • Transfer on ECMO if stable (usually day 5-10)
  • Prognosis: Moderate - dependent on listing for transplant vs LVAD as destination

Outcome 3: Failed Wean, Not Suitable for Advanced Therapies (Futile)

  • Criteria: Fails weaning AND one or more:
    • Age >75 years
    • Severe frailty
    • Irreversible neurological injury
    • Multi-organ failure
    • Absolute contraindication to transplant/LVAD
    • Patient/family wishes against escalation
  • Pathway:
    • Senior MDT meeting (ECMO consultant, intensivist, cardiologist)
    • Family meeting - discuss poor prognosis and treatment options
    • Consider time-limited trial (e.g., further 48 hours to reassess)
    • If no improvement: Withdrawal of treatment
    • Palliative care team involvement
    • Organ donation discussed if appropriate
  • Withdrawal process:
    • Family present, spiritual care if requested
    • Sedation/analgesia optimized
    • ECMO flows reduced gradually to zero
    • Death usually occurs within minutes to hours
📞 Transfer to RBH/HH Advanced Heart Failure Centre
  • Early discussion: Start discussions from day 3-4 if wean unlikely
  • Formal assessment: Virtual MDT or bedside assessment by RBH/HH team
  • Transfer logistics: Coordinated by ENS (07741123467)
  • Transfer on ECMO: Specialist retrieval team, usually by road ambulance
  • Destination: Harefield Hospital (LVAD/transplant), Royal Brompton (complex cases)

⚠️ Special Weaning Considerations

Post-Cardiotomy ECMO:

  • Often wean earlier (day 2-4) - myocardial stunning resolves quickly
  • May tolerate lower echo parameters if pre-operative function poor
  • Higher bleeding risk during decannulation
  • Sternotomy may allow easier surgical decannulation if central cannulas

E-CPR Cases:

  • Neurological outcome paramount - assess before weaning
  • Sedation hold at 48-72 hours to assess neurology (if haemodynamically stable)
  • CT head if concern for anoxic injury or ICH
  • EEG if seizure activity suspected
  • If good neurological recovery + cardiac recovery → wean as per protocol
  • If poor neurological prognosis → futility discussion

Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI-related cardiogenic shock):

  • Early revascularization essential (PCI or CABG)
  • Myocardial stunning may take 7-14 days to resolve
  • May show minimal echo improvement initially but recover well
  • Give adequate time before declaring futility

7️⃣ Section 7: Post-Cardiotomy ECMO - Specific Considerations

📊 Post-Cardiotomy ECMO Epidemiology
  • Incidence: 0.5-1.5% of all cardiac surgery cases
  • Higher risk procedures: Complex valve surgery (2-3%), redo operations (3-5%), emergency surgery (5-10%)
  • Survival to discharge: 20-40% (lower than medical cardiogenic shock ECMO)
  • Most common indication: Failure to separate from cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB)
  • Second most common: Post-operative cardiogenic shock in ICU (within 24 hours of surgery)

⚠️ Indications for Post-Cardiotomy ECMO

Primary Indication: Failure to Separate from CPB

Definition: Unable to wean from cardiopulmonary bypass despite maximal conventional support

Conventional Support Must Include ALL of:

Preload Optimization Adequate filling via CPB CVP 10-12 mmHg, adequate LAP
Inotropic Support High-dose inotropes Adrenaline 0.1-0.3 mcg/kg/min + Milrinone 0.5 mcg/kg/min
Mechanical Support IABP inserted and functioning 1:1 augmentation with good diastolic pressure

Metabolic Optimization Correct all abnormalities Ca²⁺ >1.2 mmol/L, K⁺ 4.5-5.0, Mg²⁺ >1.0, core temp >36°C

Haemodynamic Failure Criteria (Despite Above Support):

  • MAP <50 mmHg on max inotropes + IABP
  • Cardiac index <2.0 L/min/m²
  • Mixed venous saturation <50%
  • Rising lactate despite full CPB support
  • Severe biventricular dysfunction on TOE
  • Inability to reduce CPB flows below 3 L/min without deterioration
⚠️ Time-Limited Trial Before ECMO:

Before committing to ECMO, allow adequate time for myocardial recovery:

  • Minimum 30-45 minutes of maximal support on low CPB flows
  • Reassess with TOE: Any improvement in ventricular function?
  • Check surgical issues: Confirm all grafts/valves functioning, no air
  • Senior team decision: Surgeon + anaesthetist + perfusionist + ECMO consultant

Premature ECMO commitment may miss patients who would recover with more time on CPB.

Secondary Indications:

  • Post-operative cardiac arrest:
    • In operating theatre during closure
    • Within 6 hours of arrival in ICU
    • Refractory to ALS (after 10-15 minutes high-quality CPR)
    • Witnessed arrest with immediate CPR initiated
  • Progressive deterioration post-separation from CPB:
    • Initially weaned from CPB successfully
    • Deteriorating in ICU within 12-24 hours
    • Escalating inotropes/vasopressors
    • Worsening cardiac function on serial echo
  • Massive pulmonary embolism: Post-cardiac surgery (rare but catastrophic)
  • Severe RV failure: Not anticipated pre-operatively, unable to support with conventional means

🩸 Bleeding Management (HIGHEST RISK)

Post-cardiotomy ECMO has THE HIGHEST bleeding risk of all ECMO indications

Why Bleeding Risk is Extreme:

  • Surgical wound: Large sternotomy wound with extensive tissue trauma
  • CPB-induced coagulopathy:
    • Platelet dysfunction (activation and depletion)
    • Factor consumption and dilution
    • Hypothermia-induced coagulopathy
    • Heparin administration (for CPB and ECMO)
  • Mediastinal surfaces: Raw surfaces from dissection bleeding actively
  • Anticoagulation required: For ECMO circuit (conflicts with haemostasis)
  • Friable suture lines: Recent vascular anastomoses under tension

Anticoagulation Strategy (Critical Balance):

Phase 1: Immediate Post-Op (0-6 hours)

DELAY HEPARIN if at all possible

  • Accept higher circuit thrombosis risk short-term
  • NO heparin bolus
  • NO heparin infusion unless:
    • Visible clot in circuit
    • Rising pre-oxygenator pressures
    • Decreasing oxygenator function
  • Monitor chest drains VERY closely (every 15 minutes)
  • Aggressive coagulopathy correction:
    • Platelets: Target >100 × 10⁹/L (transfuse if <100)
    • Fibrinogen: Target >2.0 g/L (cryoprecipitate if low)
    • INR: Target <1.3 (FFP if elevated)
    • Temperature: Core >36.5°C (warming blankets)
  • Tranexamic acid: 1g IV (unless contraindicated)

Phase 2: Early Stabilization (6-12 hours)

Commence heparin ONLY when haemostasis improving

  • Criteria to start heparin:
    • Chest drain output <100 ml/hr × 2 hours
    • Coagulation corrected (platelets >80, fibrinogen >1.5, INR <1.5)
    • No active bleeding from surgical sites
  • Starting regimen:
    • NO loading dose
    • Start infusion ONLY at 5-10 units/kg/hr
    • Target Anti-Xa: 0.2-0.3 (LOWER than standard 0.3-0.5)
  • Monitoring: Anti-Xa every 4 hours initially

Phase 3: Late Stabilization (12-24 hours onwards)

Gradually increase to standard anticoagulation IF bleeding controlled

  • If chest drains <50 ml/hr × 12 hours:
    • Increase heparin to target Anti-Xa 0.3-0.5 (standard)
    • Monitor daily coagulation
    • Watch for occult bleeding (Hb drop, abdominal distension)
  • If bleeding persists: Remain on LOW Anti-Xa (0.2-0.3) indefinitely

🚨 Cardiac Tamponade - High Index of Suspicion

Classic signs may be masked by ECMO support! Suspect if:

  • Sudden stop or ↓ in chest drain output (drains clotted)
  • Rising CVP (>15 mmHg and climbing)
  • Falling ECMO venous drainage despite good cannula position
  • Decreasing ECMO flows (cannot achieve target flows)
  • Haemodynamic instability (↓MAP, ↑HR despite ECMO)
  • Equalization of pressures (CVP = PADP = PCWP)
  • Echo findings: Pericardial/mediastinal collection, RA/RV collapse

Action: IMMEDIATE re-exploration

Do NOT delay for confirmatory tests - clinical suspicion is enough. Take patient to theatre NOW.

Re-Exploration Procedure:

  1. Inform team: Surgeon, anaesthetist, perfusionist, theatre team
  2. Keep ECMO running throughout (do NOT stop for re-exploration)
  3. Re-sternotomy in theatre: Open sternum, evacuate clot, identify bleeding source
  4. Achieve haemostasis: Diathermy, sutures, topical haemostatic agents
  5. Washout mediastinum thoroughly
  6. Consider chest closure vs leave open: Based on haemostasis and risk of re-bleeding

⏱️ Weaning Differences from Medical ECMO

Shorter Time Course to Assessment:

  • Typical timeline: Wean assessment day 2-4 (vs day 5-7 for medical ECMO)
  • Rationale:
    • Myocardial stunning from CPB resolves quickly (24-72 hours)
    • Cardiac function often returns to pre-operative baseline
    • Longer ECMO duration in post-cardiotomy = worse prognosis
  • Earlier weaning trials justified if:
    • Bleeding controlled
    • Haemodynamically stable
    • Echo shows ANY improvement from immediate post-op

Modified Success Criteria:

Key principle: Compare to PRE-OPERATIVE function, not absolute values

Parameter Standard ECMO Target Post-Cardiotomy Modification
LVEF >20-25% May accept 15-20% if pre-op LVEF was 20-30%
LVOT VTI ≥10 cm Still critical - must be ≥10cm (non-negotiable)
Mitral S' ≥6 cm/s May accept 4-5 cm/s if pre-op function poor
Valve function No significant MR/TR Accept residual if surgically corrected (e.g., mild MR post-repair)

Focus on haemodynamic stability rather than absolute echo numbers:

  • MAP >60 mmHg on minimal vasopressors
  • Cardiac index >2.2 L/min/m²
  • No rising lactate
  • Adequate end-organ perfusion

Decannulation Approach:

Central Cannulas:

  • Location: Cardiac theatre (re-sternotomy required)
  • Procedure:
    • Re-open sternum
    • Direct removal of RA and aortic cannulas
    • Oversew cannulation sites with 4-0 Prolene running suture
    • Achieve meticulous haemostasis
    • Close sternum with wires
  • Risk: Higher bleeding risk - ensure excellent coagulation before proceeding

Peripheral Cannulas:

  • As per standard protocol (see Section 6)
  • Femoral artery/vein surgical closure or manual pressure
  • DPC removal with manual pressure

Sternal Closure Considerations:

  • Delayed closure common: 2-5 days post-ECMO due to:
    • Myocardial/mediastinal oedema
    • Risk of cardiac compression if closed early
    • Ongoing bleeding risk
  • Criteria for safe closure:
    • Minimal chest drain output (<50ml/hr × 24hrs)
    • Haemodynamically stable off ECMO
    • Coagulation normalized
    • Mediastinal oedema resolved

🎯 Prognostic Factors & Decision-Making

Good Prognostic Indicators:

  • Short CPB time: <3 hours total
  • Identifiable, correctable cause:
    • Coronary graft failure amenable to revision
    • Air embolism (resolves with time)
    • Inadequate myocardial protection (reversible stunning)
  • Age <65 years
  • First-time cardiac surgery (not redo)
  • No pre-operative renal failure
  • Minimal bleeding: No re-exploration required
  • Early cardiac recovery: Echo improvement within 48-72 hours
  • Isolated LV failure: (RV function preserved)

Poor Prognostic Indicators:

  • Prolonged CPB time: >5 hours
  • Emergency surgery (vs elective)
  • Redo cardiac surgery: Especially 3rd or 4th operation
  • Severe biventricular failure: Both LV and RV failing
  • Uncontrolled bleeding: Multiple re-explorations required
  • Pre-existing end-organ dysfunction: Renal failure, liver disease
  • Age >75 years
  • No cardiac recovery by day 3-4
⚠️ Futility Assessment (Earlier than Medical ECMO):

Post-cardiotomy ECMO has worse prognosis than medical ECMO. Consider futility discussion if:

  • Day 3-4: No cardiac recovery AND poor prognostic factors
  • Multiple re-explorations for bleeding with ongoing transfusion requirement
  • Multi-organ failure developing (renal, hepatic, neurological)
  • Age >75 with no recovery

Earlier withdrawal discussion appropriate vs prolonging inevitable poor outcome.

📍 Destination Planning

Location During ECMO:

  1. First 48-72 hours: C604 (Cardiac ICU)
    • High likelihood of return to theatre for bleeding
    • Immediate cardiothoracic surgical access essential
    • Chest may be open
    • Close monitoring of chest drains
  2. After 48-72 hours if stable: Decision point
    • Improving + bleeding controlled → Remain C604 until successful wean
    • Stable but not improving → Consider transfer to A600 OR discuss RBH/HH transfer
    • Deteriorating/futile → Senior MDT discussion regarding withdrawal

Long-Term Outcomes & Destinations:

  • Successful wean (30-40%):
    • Decannulation in cardiac theatre
    • Remain C604 until extubated and haemodynamically stable
    • Transfer to cardiac ward when appropriate
    • Standard post-cardiac surgery recovery pathway
    • Outpatient cardiology follow-up
  • Failed wean but suitable for advanced therapies (10-15%):
    • Transfer to RBH/HH for assessment
    • Options: LVAD, transplant evaluation
    • Requires age <65, good functional status, no contraindications
  • Futile - withdrawal of treatment (50-60%):
    • Senior MDT discussion (cardiac surgeon + ECMO consultant + intensivist)
    • Family meeting - discuss poor prognosis
    • Palliative care involvement
    • Withdrawal protocol as per Section 6
    • Often occurs day 5-7 if no recovery

How to Classify a Patient (Step-by-Step):

  1. Assess for hypoperfusion signs:
    • Mental status (alert vs confused vs unresponsive)
    • Skin perfusion (warm vs cool vs mottled)
    • Capillary refill (CRT <2s vs >2s)
    • Urine output (>30 ml/hr vs oliguric vs anuric)
  2. Measure haemodynamics:
    • SBP, HR, lactate (essential)
    • Cardiac index if PA catheter in situ
    • SvO₂ if available
  3. Assess current support level:
    • None vs single agent vs multiple agents
    • IABP present?
    • Trending better, stable, or worse?
  4. Classify according to worst parameter:
    • If ANY feature meets higher stage → classify as higher stage
    • Example: Normal lactate BUT Stage E cardiac arrest = Stage E
  5. Re-assess frequently:
    • SCAI stage is DYNAMIC - can improve or worsen
    • Re-classify every 2-4 hours or with significant change

📋 SCAI Classification in Clinical Practice

Documentation:

  • Include SCAI stage in all ICU notes for cardiogenic shock patients
  • Format: "SCAI D cardiogenic shock secondary to acute anterior STEMI"
  • Update stage if patient improves or deteriorates

Communication:

  • Handovers: "Patient is SCAI D shock, consider ECMO"
  • MDT discussions: Use SCAI stage to standardize severity assessment
  • Inter-hospital transfers: Quote SCAI stage to receiving team

⚠️ Limitations of SCAI Classification

  • Not validated for all aetiologies: Developed primarily for AMI-CS, may not perfectly apply to other causes
  • Subjective elements: Mental status, perfusion assessment require clinical judgment
  • Dynamic classification: Patient can move between stages rapidly - requires frequent reassessment
  • Does not replace clinical judgment: Use as guide, not absolute rule
  • Overlap between stages: Some patients have features of multiple stages - classify as worst

📎 Appendix 3: E-CPR Activation Algorithm

📚 E-CPR Definition

Extracorporeal Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (E-CPR): The use of V-A ECMO during ongoing CPR for refractory cardiac arrest when conventional ALS has failed.

Goal: Provide circulatory and respiratory support whilst reversible causes are addressed

Appendix 3: E-CPR activation algorithm for BRI/BHI - integrates with standard ALS protocol

🚨 GOLDEN RULE: Minimize Low-Flow Time

Low-flow time = Duration of CPR (from arrest to ECMO flows)

  • <30 minutes: Best outcomes (50-60% survival with good neuro)
  • 30-45 minutes: Acceptable (30-40% survival)
  • 45-60 minutes: Poor prognosis (10-20% survival)
  • >60 minutes: Futile in most cases (<5% good neuro outcome)

Therefore: EARLY decision and RAPID cannulation are essential

✅ E-CPR Eligibility (Rapid Assessment at 15 minutes)

Inclusion Criteria (ALL required):

  • ✓ Witnessed arrest (in-hospital OR OHCA with bystander CPR)
  • ✓ Age 18-70 years (consider up to 75 if excellent baseline)
  • ✓ Shockable rhythm (VF/pVT) OR presumed cardiac cause
  • ✓ No ROSC after 3 cycles ALS (approximately 15 minutes CPR)
  • ✓ High-quality CPR maintained (end-tidal CO₂ >10 mmHg)
  • ✓ Reversible cause likely (e.g., acute MI, PE, hyperK⁺, toxicology)
  • ✓ No STOP criteria (see below)

STOP Criteria (ANY = Do NOT proceed):

Clinical STOP Criteria (ANY = STOP):

  • ⛔ Unwitnessed arrest with unknown downtime
  • ⛔ No bystander CPR for OHCA
  • ⛔ Non-shockable rhythm throughout (unless clear reversible cause like massive PE)
  • ⛔ Known DNAR/ceiling of treatment
  • ⛔ Terminal illness or severe frailty (CFS ≥7)
  • ⛔ Major trauma or uncontrolled bleeding
  • ⛔ Known severe aortic regurgitation (AR contraindication to VA-ECMO)
  • ⛔ Known irreversible brain injury

Physiological STOP Criteria (≥2 = STOP):

  • 📉 pH <7.0 despite high-quality CPR
  • 📉 Lactate >15 mmol/L
  • 📉 End-tidal CO₂ <10 mmHg consistently (suggests inadequate CPR or non-viable)
  • 📉 Pupils fixed and dilated bilaterally (may indicate anoxic brain injury)
  • 📉 No respiratory effort despite attempts to ventilate
⚠️ Grey Areas - Seek Senior Input:
  • Age 70-75: Assess baseline function - if independent, active, no comorbidities → consider
  • Downtime 10-15 minutes before CPR: Unlikely to succeed but discuss with ECMO consultant
  • PEA throughout: If clear reversible cause (e.g., massive PE confirmed on echo) → consider
  • Initial non-shockable converting to VF: May still be viable - discuss

📞 E-CPR Activation Process

Step 1: Team Leader Assessment (at 15 minutes / 3 cycles ALS)

  1. Pause briefly (continue CPR but brief discussion)
    • "We've completed 3 cycles of ALS without ROSC"
    • "This patient meets criteria for E-CPR consideration"
  2. Rapidly check eligibility:
    • Inclusion criteria met? (witnessed, age, rhythm, reversible cause)
    • ANY stop criteria? (If yes → do NOT activate)
  3. Decision: Activate E-CPR pathway YES / NO

Step 2: Activation Call (Immediate)

Team leader or designated person calls:

  • In hours (08:00-17:00):
    • Bleep ICU Outreach Registrar: Ext 27139
    • OR Contact switchboard: 0117 923 0000 "ECMO consultant for E-CPR activation"
  • Out of hours:
    • Put out cardiac arrest call extension: 2222
    • Inform "ECMO consultant needed for E-CPR - please activate team"

Information to provide:

  • Location of arrest
  • Age and brief history
  • Witnessed? Bystander CPR?
  • Rhythm (shockable vs non-shockable)
  • Duration of CPR so far
  • ETCO₂ values (confirms CPR quality)
  • Presumed cause

Step 3: Continue CPR Whilst Team Assembles

CRITICAL: High-quality CPR must continue WITHOUT interruption

  • DO NOT stop CPR whilst waiting for ECMO team
  • Maintain compressions:
    • Rate 100-120 per minute
    • Depth 5-6 cm
    • Full chest recoil
    • Minimize interruptions (<10 seconds for rhythm checks)
  • Monitor ETCO₂: Target >10 mmHg (confirms adequate CPR)
  • Rotate compressors every 2 minutes (prevent fatigue)
  • Continue ALS: Adrenaline, defibrillation as per protocol

Step 4: Prepare for Cannulation

  • Location decision:
    • Cardiac arrest in ICU → Cannulate at bedside
    • Cardiac arrest in cath lab → Cannulate in cath lab (ideal - fluoroscopy available)
    • Cardiac arrest elsewhere → Transfer to ICU/cath lab if safe (maintain CPR during transfer)
  • Prepare groins:
    • Expose both groins fully
    • Ultrasound femoral vessels (identify arteries/veins)
    • Prep and drape ONE groin sterile (continue CPR on opposite side)
  • Equipment ready:
    • ECMO circuit primed (perfusionist)
    • Cannulation set opened
    • Ultrasound with sterile probe cover

Step 5: Cannulation During CPR

  1. Femoral venous access: Ultrasound-guided, continue CPR
  2. Femoral arterial access: Ultrasound-guided or palpate femoral pulse from compressions
  3. Cannula insertion: As per Section 3 protocol (venous first, then arterial, then DPC)
  4. Connect to circuit: Venous and arterial cannulas
  5. Initiate flows: Start ECMO pump
    • Begin at 1-2 L/min
    • Gradually increase to 4-5 L/min
    • STOP CHEST COMPRESSIONS once flows >3 L/min and MAP >50 mmHg
  6. Insert DPC: Once off CPR, insert distal perfusion cannula as per protocol

🎯 Post-ECMO Initiation Priorities

Immediate (0-1 hour):

  1. Confirm ECMO function:
    • Adequate flows (4-5 L/min)
    • MAP 60-70 mmHg
    • Post-oxy PaO₂ >200 mmHg
    • Venous drainage good (not chattering)
  2. Address underlying cause:
    • STEMI: Urgent PCI (can be performed on ECMO)
    • PE: Thrombolysis or thrombectomy
    • Electrolyte: Correct K⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺
    • Toxicology: Specific antidotes, prolonged ECMO for drug clearance
  3. Neurological assessment:
    • Pupil reactivity
    • Motor responses (if not paralyzed)
    • GCS when sedation light
    • DO NOT prognosticate in first 24-48 hours
  4. Cerebral monitoring:
    • Apply bilateral cerebral NIRS
    • Target >55% bilaterally
    • Significant asymmetry (>10%) → urgent CT head

Early (2-48 hours):

  • Sedation hold: At 48-72 hours if haemodynamically stable
    • Assess neurological recovery
    • Pupil reactivity, motor responses, GCS
  • Neuroimaging:
    • CT head if concern for anoxic injury or ICH
    • MRI brain at 72-96 hours for prognostication if indicated
  • Prognostication:
    • Wait MINIMUM 72 hours before neurological prognostication
    • Multi-modal assessment (clinical exam, imaging, EEG, SSEP)
    • Involve neurology for formal assessment

📎 Appendix 4: Cerebral NIRS Monitoring & Management

🧠 Why Cerebral NIRS on V-A ECMO?

Cerebral Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (NIRS) monitors brain oxygenation continuously and non-invasively during ECMO support.

Critical for detecting:

  • Harlequin syndrome (differential hypoxaemia)
  • Cerebral hypoperfusion
  • Stroke or intracranial haemorrhage
  • Inadequate ECMO flows

Appendix 4: Cerebral NIRS monitoring thresholds and management algorithm for V-A ECMO patients

🎯 Cerebral NIRS Monitoring Principles

Technology:

  • Device: Typically INVOS or similar cerebral oximeter
  • Sensors: Bilateral frontal lobe placement (right and left forehead)
  • Measurement: Regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rSO₂) as percentage
  • Continuous: Real-time monitoring, alarms for low values

Application:

  1. Clean and dry bilateral forehead skin
  2. Apply NIRS sensors symmetrically above eyebrows
  3. Secure sensors with adhesive
  4. Connect to monitor and establish baseline
  5. Set alarms for <55% or >10% drop from baseline

Normal Values:

rSO₂ Value Interpretation Action
60-80% Normal cerebral oxygenation Continue current management
55-60% Low-normal (concern) Optimize ECMO, check patient status
<55% Cerebral hypoxia Urgent intervention required
Drop >10-15% from baseline Acute deterioration Immediate assessment and intervention
⚠️ Asymmetry is Important

>10% difference between left and right suggests:

  • Unilateral stroke (ischaemic or haemorrhagic)
  • Sensor malposition (check and reposition)
  • Cannula malposition affecting cerebral flow

Action: Check sensor position, then urgent CT head if asymmetry persists

🚨 Low Cerebral NIRS Management Algorithm

When rSO₂ <55% or Drop >10% from Baseline:

STEP 1: Exclude Technical Issues (30 seconds)

  • ✓ Sensors properly positioned? (reposition if needed)
  • ✓ Good contact with skin? (no hair, moisture, movement)
  • ✓ Bilateral sensors reading? (not just unilateral artifact)

If sensors OK → Proceed to Step 2

STEP 2: Optimize Oxygen Delivery (Immediate)

  1. Check right radial ABG:
    • PaO₂, SaO₂ (confirms if this is Harlequin syndrome)
    • If right radial PaO₂ <8 kPa → Harlequin syndrome (see Section 5)
  2. Increase ventilator FiO₂:
    • ↑ to 1.0 immediately
    • Improves native lung oxygenation
  3. Increase ECMO support:
    • ↑ ECMO flows to maximum tolerated (4.5-6 L/min)
    • ↑ FdO₂ (sweep gas) to 1.0
    • Check post-oxygenator gas (should be PaO₂ >300 mmHg)
  4. Optimize haemodynamics:
    • Target MAP 65-70 mmHg (may need vasopressors)
    • Ensure adequate Hb (>90 g/L for oxygen carrying capacity)

Reassess NIRS after 5-10 minutes

STEP 3: If No Improvement → Advanced Assessment

  • Urgent TOE/TTE:
    • Check cannula positions (venous in RA, arterial in descending aorta)
    • Assess cardiac function and aortic flow pattern
    • Look for intracardiac thrombus or air
  • Check for Harlequin syndrome:
    • Right hand SpO₂ vs lower limb SpO₂ (differential?)
    • Right radial ABG vs femoral ABG (PaO₂ difference?)
    • If confirmed → Harlequin protocol (Section 5)
  • Consider stroke:
    • Especially if unilateral NIRS drop
    • Clinical exam when able (pupils, motor responses)
    • Urgent CT head if concern

STEP 4: Escalation (if refractory to Steps 1-3)

  • Urgent CT head: Rule out ICH, stroke, mass effect
  • ECMO consultant review: Immediate reassessment
  • Consider advanced interventions:
    • Upper body cannulation (for Harlequin syndrome)
    • Cannula repositioning if malpositioned
    • Neurosurgery consult if ICH identified

📋 Specific Clinical Scenarios

Scenario 1: Bilateral Low NIRS + Low Right Radial PaO₂

Diagnosis: Harlequin syndrome

Action: See Section 5 - Harlequin management protocol

  • Optimize native lung function (↑FiO₂, ↑PEEP, recruitment)
  • Maximize ECMO flows
  • If refractory → upper body cannulation or VV conversion

Scenario 2: Bilateral Low NIRS + Normal Right Radial PaO₂

Diagnosis: Global cerebral hypoperfusion (not Harlequin)

Causes:

  • Inadequate ECMO flows
  • Low MAP (<50 mmHg)
  • Severe anaemia
  • Cerebral oedema

Action:

  • ↑ ECMO flows to maximum
  • ↑ MAP to 65-70 mmHg (vasopressors if needed)
  • Transfuse if Hb <90 g/L
  • CT head to exclude mass effect/oedema

Scenario 3: Unilateral Low NIRS (Left vs Right >10% Difference)

Diagnosis: Likely unilateral stroke OR sensor issue

Action:

  1. Check and reposition sensors (rule out technical issue)
  2. If persistent asymmetry → URGENT CT head
  3. Likely findings:
    • Ischaemic stroke (territory infarct)
    • Haemorrhagic stroke (ICH)
    • Subdural haematoma
  4. Neurology/neurosurgery consult
  5. Anticoagulation decision (stop if ICH, continue with caution if ischaemic)

Scenario 4: Sudden Drop in NIRS (was >60%, now <50%)

Diagnosis: Acute event

Causes:

  • Circuit malfunction (pump failure, cannula displacement)
  • Acute massive PE
  • Cardiac arrest
  • Massive ICH

Action:

  • ABC assessment (airway, breathing, circulation)
  • Check ECMO circuit (flows, pressures, alarms)
  • Urgent echo (cardiac function, tamponade, PE?)
  • Urgent CT head if not explained by above

⚠️ Limitations of Cerebral NIRS

  • Not specific: Low NIRS doesn't tell you WHY (need clinical correlation)
  • Regional only: Measures frontal lobes only (may miss posterior circulation strokes)
  • Affected by: Scalp oedema, skull thickness, sensor position
  • No absolute threshold: Trending and changes more important than single value
  • Cannot diagnose stroke: CT/MRI required for definitive diagnosis

Despite limitations, cerebral NIRS is EXTREMELY valuable for early detection of problems on VA-ECMO
