Appendix 2: SCAI Grading of Cardiogenic Shock

Classification system for severity of cardiogenic shock

Reference: Baran DA, Grines CL, Bailey S et al. SCAI clinical expert consensus statement on the classification of cardiogenic shock. Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions 2019; 94: 29-37.

SCAI Shock Classification System

SCAI Stage Description Physical Exam Biochemical Markers Hemodynamics
A
At Risk 		Not currently experiencing signs or symptoms of CS, but at risk for its development
  • Normal JVP
  • Warm and well-perfused
  • Strong distal pulses
  • Normal mentation
  • Clear lung sounds
  • Normal lactate
  • Normal (or at baseline) renal function
  • SBP >100mmHg (or at baseline)
  • CI ≥2.5 (if acute)
  • CVP <10
  • PCWP ≤15
  • PA Sat ≥65%
B
Beginning 		Clinical evidence of hemodynamic instability without hypoperfusion
  • Elevated JVP
  • Warm and well-perfused
  • Strong distal pulses
  • Normal mentation
  • Rales in lung fields
  • Normal lactate
  • Minimal acute renal function impairment
  • Elevated BNP
  • SBP <90mmHg or MAP <60mmHg, or >30mm drop from baseline
  • HR ≥100bpm
C
Classic CS 		Hypoperfusion requiring intervention (pharmacologic or mechanical) beyond volume resuscitation
  • Volume overload
  • Altered mental status
  • Cold and clammy
  • Extensive rales
  • Urine output <30mL/h
  • Lactate ≥2
  • CR 1.5 x baseline or ≥50% drop in GFR
  • Increased LFTs
  • Elevated BNP
  • CI <2.2 (if invasive hemodynamics performed)
  • Altered mentation, cool extremities
  • PCWP >15
D
Deteriorating 		Failure of initial support strategy to restore perfusion
  • Any of stage C and worsening (or not improving) signs/sx of hypoperfusion despite initial therapy
  • Any of stage C and lactate rising and persistently ≥2
  • Deteriorating renal function
  • Worsening LFTs
  • Rising BNP
  • Any of stage C and requiring escalating doses or increasing numbers of pressors or addition of MCS device to maintain perfusion
E
Extremis 		Acute or impending circulatory collapse
  • Typically unconscious
  • Near pulselessness
  • Cardiac collapse
  • Multiple defibrillations
  • Lactate ≥8
  • CPR
  • Severe acidosis (pH <7.2)
  • Profound hypotension despite maximal hemodynamic support
  • Need for bolus doses of vasopressors

Note: The stepped assessment allows rapid clinical classification. Start at Step 1 and work down until criteria are met.

Clinical Application for VA-ECMO

ECMO Candidacy: VA-ECMO is typically considered for patients in SCAI D or E shock who have failed optimal conventional therapy.

Key Decision Points

  • Stage B (Beginning): Optimize conventional therapy, monitor closely for progression
  • Stage C (Classic): Consider for ECMO if optimal conventional therapy fails
  • Stage D (Deteriorating): Strong candidate for ECMO if inclusion criteria met
  • Stage E (Extremis): E-CPR consideration if arrest occurs, ECMO if pre-arrest and suitable

Modifier: Cardiac Arrest

The SCAI classification includes an "A" modifier for cardiac arrest with concern for anoxic brain injury. This significantly impacts prognosis and should be considered in decision-making.
