LV Distention & Loss of Arterial Pulsatility

Recognition and Management

LV Distention Syndrome

LV distention in VA-ECMO occurs as part of LV loading and as a combination of factors:

  • The intrinsic cardiac pathology
  • Reduced native cardiac function, inadequate aortic valve opening and lack of LV output
  • Increased afterload from retrograde VA-ECMO flow and tightly squeezed arterial system (exacerbated by aortic regurgitation)

Consequences of LV Distention

Distention leads to raised LVEDP and LV blood stasis, causing:

  • Mitral annular distention and mitral regurgitation → pulmonary congestion and worsening native gas exchange
  • Worsening LV loading (vicious cycle) → poor native cardiac recovery
  • LV/LA electrical system disruption → arrhythmias
  • Increased risk of intra-cardiac thrombus formation (LV/LVOT/aortic root)
  • Back pressure through the RH → end organ venous congestion despite adequate arterial flow

Detection

EMERGENCY: Once LV distention is recognised, urgent unloading strategies should be undertaken (<2hrs to achieve full unloading).

1. Echocardiography

Required immediately after ECMO initiation and then daily, with further repeat studies if clinical features suggest LV distention.

Look for:

  • LVIDd >6.0cm or LVEDV/BSA >100ml/m² (or significant increase between scans)
  • Very severely reduced LV ejection (LVEF <10%, LVOT VTI <5cm)
  • Aortic valve opening less than every 3-4 cardiac contractions
  • Greater than mild aortic or mitral regurgitation
  • Moderate to severe spontaneous LV echo contrast or LV thrombus

2. Clinical Markers

  • PAPs via PAC: PADP or PCWP >18mmHg
  • SvO₂ <55%
  • Arterial pulse pressure <10mmHg
  • Worsening gas exchange with pulmonary oedema on CXR
  • New arrhythmias, especially ventricular
  • Worsening shock indices despite escalating VA-ECMO flows

Treatment

Medical Management

Reduce Afterload:

  • Ensure MAP 55-60mmHg, consider reducing to 50-55mmHg
  • Wean vasopressors or introduce vasodilators
  • Trial of REDUCING VA-ECMO flows (ensuring shock indices don't deteriorate)

Reduce Back Pressure:

  • Increase PEEP (15 to 25 cmH₂O) - short term therapy only
  • Volume state reduction (fluid removal) if BP and venous pressures adequate
  • Trial of INCREASED VA-ECMO flows (where RV function better than LV function)

Increase LV Contractility:

  • Inotropic support for maintenance of pulsatility
  • Note: Inotropes may exacerbate LV distention if LV is non-responsive by primarily increasing RV output

Mechanical LV Decompression ("LV Venting")

a) Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP)

Provides afterload reduction and may assist patients at risk of LV distention, however NOT considered treatment for progressive LV distention uncontrolled by medical management.

b) Impella CP (Percutaneous LVAD)

Provides direct decompression of the LV.

c) Pulmonary Arterial Drainage

Via single lumen access cannula placed in the PA.

d) Surgical Options

  • Trans-apical LV vent cannula
  • Atrial septostomy +/- LA vent cannula
