Loss of Arterial Pulsatility

Assessment and Management of Native Cardiac Function

Overview

Arterial pulsatility reflects native cardiac function on ECMO support. Maintenance of native cardiac function is a primary aim of V-A ECMO support.

Critical Point: Loss of arterial pulsatility increases risk of LV distention syndrome and should prompt immediate assessment and intervention.

Note: In ECPR and more severe forms of acute cardiac failure, it is common to lose pulsatility immediately after commencing V-A ECMO. This requires particularly vigilant monitoring and early LV venting strategies.

Causes

Common Etiologies

  • Artefact: Dampened arterial line transduction
  • Inadequate preload: Excessive V-A ECMO flows, haemorrhage, hypovolaemia
  • Mechanical obstruction:
    • Cardiac tamponade
    • Tension pneumothorax
    • RVOT/LVOT obstruction
    • Pulmonary embolism
    • Intrathoracic bleeding causing compression (common post-cardiotomy)
  • Arrhythmias: Non-perfusing or poorly perfusing rhythms
  • LV failure:
    • Poor contractility (ischaemia/infarction)
    • Excessive afterload (typical MAP target 50-55 mmHg)
    • Valvular regurgitation (aortic or mitral)

Consequences

  • Thrombosis risk:
    • Massive intracardiac thrombosis (generally lethal)
    • Thromboembolism → cerebral, limb, kidney or gut ischaemia/infarction
  • Increased LV filling pressure: High LV load and distention
  • Impaired coronary perfusion: Further myocardial ischaemic damage
  • Mitral regurgitation: Leads to LA hypertension, pulmonary oedema, pulmonary haemorrhage

Assessment

Initial Checks

  1. Arterial line: Check for damping, palpable peripheral pulses, consider line replacement
  2. Rhythm assessment: Exclude non-perfusing arrhythmias
  3. ECMO parameters: Check flow and pump speed, assess for access insufficiency
  4. Bleeding: Clinical examination and trending Hb for occult blood loss
  5. Pneumothorax: Ventilation pressures/volumes, auscultation, CXR/USS
  6. Tamponade: Bedside echo, measure CVP
  7. Pulmonary oedema: ETT secretions (frothy ± haemorrhage) or CXR findings

Echocardiographic Assessment

Essential for determining cause and guiding management:

  • Tamponade detection: Presence and size of pericardial collection
  • Left heart: LV size, function, compression/obstruction, SEC ("smoke") or thrombus
  • Right heart: RV size, function, compression/obstruction, SEC or thrombus
  • Valves: Mitral/aortic regurgitation (may be continuous on VA-ECMO)
  • Aortic valve opening: Frequency and adequacy

Management

Management depends on underlying cause identified during assessment:

Inadequate Preload

  • Consider volume loading (including blood products)
  • Caution: LV failure with distention usually shows "sucked down" RA/IVC
  • Volume loading will NOT correct LV distention
  • Need for volume in VA-ECMO should trigger search for LV failure and bleeding

Excessive Afterload

  • Review MAP targets (consider reducing to 50-55 mmHg)
  • Consider inodilators/vasodilators
  • Apply PEEP (increases intrathoracic pressure)
  • Note: Aggressive afterload control essential in these situations

Mechanical Obstruction

  • Usually requires surgical management
  • Adjunctive medical management (e.g., volume loading as temporising measure)
  • Correct coagulopathy preoperatively if tamponade present

Poor Contractility

  • Treat underlying cause (e.g., angiography for ischaemia)
  • Optimize: temperature, acidosis, pO₂, pCO₂

Arrhythmia

  • Aim for K⁺ >4.5, Mg²⁺ >1 (higher for ventricular arrhythmias)
  • Consider amiodarone / direct current cardioversion (DCR)
  • β-blockade may be used after senior consultation

RV/LV Failure

  • Consider inodilators or constrictors (e.g., milrinone, adrenaline)
  • MAP-guided choice
  • Pulmonary vasodilators (e.g., iNO) if indicated
LV Venting Required: Loss of pulsatility with pulmonary oedema indicates need for:
  1. LV venting device insertion (if not already in situ)
  2. Increased anticoagulation targets (especially with SEC or thrombus)
