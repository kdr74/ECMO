Haematological Management: Bleeding & TEG

Coagulation Management and Bleeding Protocol on VA-ECMO

Overview

Many patients will require anti-platelet therapy for their cardiac pathology, as well as anticoagulation for the mechanical support. Balancing the bleeding and thrombosis risks is a critical aspect of VA-ECMO management.

Pre-requisites for Anticoagulation:
  • Arrest of significant peripheral bleeding from line sites
  • CT excluding significant intra-cranial or chest/abdominal bleeding (if stable enough for CT)

Standard Anticoagulation Protocol

Initiation

Once bleeding excluded, start unfractionated heparin infusion:

  • Target Anti-Xa: 0.3-0.5 IU/mL
  • Monitoring: At least twice daily clotting screens including fibrinogen
  • Valid crossmatch: Ensure available at all times

Target Parameters

  • Haemoglobin: Keep >80-90 g/L
  • Platelets: Keep >50 × 10⁹/L (>75 if bleeding)
  • Fibrinogen: Keep >1.5 g/L (>2.0 if bleeding)
  • INR: <1.3 if bleeding
Transfusion Threshold: Low threshold for investigation of occult bleeding if >1 unit RBC transfusion required per day.

Bleeding Management Protocol

If Significant Bleeding Occurs:

1. Perform TEG for Rapid Assessment

TEG provides rapid identification of systemic clotting abnormalities and guides product administration.

2. Optimize Coagulation Parameters

  • Repeat formal lab tests
  • Target fibrinogen: >2.0 g/L
  • Target platelets: >75 × 10⁹/L
  • Target INR: <1.3

3. Stop Heparin and Consider Reversal

  • Stop heparin infusion immediately
  • Consider up to ¼ dose protamine reversal
  • CAUTION: High risk of clotting in ECMO circuit

4. Administer Tranexamic Acid (TXA) Infusion

Preparation:
  • Prepare 50 mL syringe with "neat" TXA solution (5g total)
Dosing:
  • Loading dose: 12.5 mg/kg over 30 minutes
  • Maintenance: 6.5 mg/kg/hour immediately after loading dose
  • Continue until syringe empty (~10 hours) or bleeding ceased for 2 hours
  • Extended infusion: Reduce to 3 mg/kg/hr if continuing beyond 5 grams
Dose Adjustment:
  • Reduce maintenance rate if renal or hepatic dysfunction present
  • Loading dose remains unchanged
Main Risk: Acute thrombotic events, including circuit thrombosis

5. Local Haemostasis Techniques

  • Interventional radiology procedures
  • Surgical interventions as needed
  • Direct pressure and local measures

6. Factor VIIa (Last Resort)

  • Only if bleeding persists despite correction of all above areas
  • Must discuss with haematology first
  • Very high thrombosis risk

TEG Interpretation

Key TEG Parameters

  • R time (Reaction time): Time to clot initiation
    • Prolonged → Factor deficiency or heparin effect
  • K time and α angle: Clot kinetics
    • Prolonged K / decreased α → Low fibrinogen
  • MA (Maximum amplitude): Clot strength
    • Decreased MA → Thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction
  • LY30 (Lysis at 30 min): Fibrinolysis
    • Elevated → Hyperfibrinolysis (consider TXA)

TEG-Guided Therapy

  • Prolonged R: FFP, consider protamine if heparinized
  • Prolonged K/low α: Cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate
  • Low MA: Platelet transfusion
  • Elevated LY30: Tranexamic acid

Post-Cardiotomy Considerations

  • Higher bleeding risk: Surgical site, coagulopathy from CPB
  • Delayed heparinization: Circuit run at high flows (>3 L/min) without heparin for first 12-48 hours
  • Early TEG use: To guide product replacement
  • Cell salvage: May be utilized if significant chest drainage
