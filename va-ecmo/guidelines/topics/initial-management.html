Initial Management Post-Instigation of VA-ECMO

Immediate Care and Stabilisation in the First 24-48 Hours

Overview

The immediate period following VA-ECMO cannulation is critical. The goals are to stabilize the patient, optimize end-organ perfusion, prevent complications, and begin early steps toward myocardial recovery. This requires coordinated multidisciplinary care with careful attention to multiple organ systems simultaneously.

Time-Critical Period: The first 24-48 hours on VA-ECMO determine outcomes. Early recognition and management of complications, combined with proactive optimization, significantly improve survival.

Immediate Post-Cannulation Priorities

Location and Transfer Pathway

Following ECMO cannulation, the patient typically requires movement through multiple locations:

  1. Location of cannulation:
    • Establish monitoring and relative stabilization
    • Insert distal perfusion cannula (DPC)
    • Baseline TTE or TOE to assess LV distention, aortic valve opening, cannula position
  2. Cardiac catheterization lab (if indicated):
    • Diagnostic coronary angiogram ± PCI
    • LV venting device insertion (Impella or IABP) if LV distention present
    • DPC insertion if not performed at cannulation
    • May be delayed up to 12 hours if: no indication for LV vent, clear non-coronary pathology, DPC in situ
  3. CT imaging (if stable):
    • CT head (especially if OHCA), CT neck (if OHCA), CTPA, CT aortogram, CT abdomen/pelvis
    • Rule out bleeding complications, guide diagnostics
    • May need to be delayed until ICU admission if unstable
  4. ICU admission:
    • A600 (GICU) for stabilization and ongoing care
    • Post-cardiotomy patients → C604 (CICU) for first 48-72 hours

Essential Interventions

Monitoring and Vascular Access

Arterial Access - CRITICAL

  • Right radial or right brachial arterial line - MANDATORY
    • Monitors upper body oxygenation (cerebral and coronary perfusion)
    • Detects Harlequin syndrome (differential hypoxia)
    • Target right arm PaO₂ >8-10 kPa, SpO₂ >88-92%

Central Venous Access

  • Left internal jugular (LIJ) preferred
    • Leaves right IJ available for pulmonary artery catheter or vascath
    • Triple lumen or quad lumen
    • For vasoactive infusions, fluids, antibiotics

Pulmonary Artery Catheter (PAC)

  • Insert once on ICU (via right IJ typically)
  • Provides essential data:
    • Cardiac output measurement
    • Pulmonary artery pressures (PAP)
    • Pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) - surrogate for LVEDP
    • Mixed venous saturation (SvO₂)
  • Guides management:
    • PADP or PCWP >18 mmHg suggests LV distention
    • Low SvO₂ <60% despite adequate ECMO flow suggests inadequate perfusion

NIRS Monitoring - STANDARD OF CARE

  • 4-sensor minimum:
    • Bilateral cerebral (forehead)
    • Bilateral lower limb (calves)
  • Purpose: Early detection of Harlequin syndrome and limb ischaemia
  • See NIRS Monitoring guideline for full details

Distal Perfusion Cannula (DPC)

  • Insert at time of cannulation or within 2-4 hours
  • Universal recommendation for all peripheral VA-ECMO patients
  • See Distal Limb Perfusion guideline for technique

Echocardiography - MANDATORY

  • Timing: Level 2 scan prior to cath lab (or as soon as possible post-cannulation)
  • Key assessments:
    • LV distension: LVIDd >6.0 cm, LVEF <10%, LVOT VTI <5 cm, inadequate AV opening
    • Aortic valve opening: Frequency and adequacy
    • Biventricular function: LV and RV systolic function
    • Cannula position: Venous cannula in RA/SVC, no pericardial tamponade
    • Valvular function: Especially aortic and mitral regurgitation
  • Frequency: Daily minimum, more frequently if concerns about LV distention

Left Ventricular Venting

Indication: Evidence of LV distention despite optimization of ECMO flows and inotropic support

Options:

  • Intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP): Afterload reduction, prophylactic role
  • Impella CP (preferred): Direct LV decompression + circulatory support
  • Pulmonary artery drainage: Via single-lumen cannula in PA
  • Surgical options: Trans-apical LV vent, atrial septostomy

See LV Distention guideline for full management algorithm

Coronary Angiography

  • Timing: Within 12 hours of ECMO initiation (unless clear non-coronary pathology)
  • Include: LVEDP measurement
  • Consider PCI: If culprit lesion identified and feasible

CT Imaging

Comprehensive imaging to rule out complications and guide diagnosis:

  • CT head: Rule out intracranial haemorrhage (especially post-OHCA)
  • CT neck: If OHCA with intubation (rule out vascular injury)
  • CTPA: Rule out pulmonary embolism, assess lung parenchyma
  • CT aortogram: Rule out aortic dissection, cannula position
  • CT abdomen/pelvis: Rule out bleeding, assess visceral perfusion

Timing: Prior to ICU admission if stable, or within first 24-48 hours

Physiological Targets

Ventilation

Goal: Lung-protective ventilation while maintaining adequate native gas exchange

Settings:

  • Mode: BiPAP (or pressure-controlled ventilation)
  • Initial settings: "ECMO rest" (PInsp 10, PEEP 10, RR 10)
  • Tidal volume: 6-8 ml/kg predicted body weight
  • Driving pressure: <14 cmH₂O
  • PEEP: Optimize (typically 10-15 cmH₂O)
    • Balance lung recruitment vs RV afterload
    • Consider LV distention when setting PEEP
  • FiO₂: Target right arm SpO₂ 94-98% / PaO₂ >8 kPa
    • Native lung gas exchange essential to prevent Harlequin syndrome

Key Principles:

  • Decarboxylation: Achieved by ECMO sweep gas flow, NOT ventilator minute ventilation
  • Avoid hyperventilation: Low PaCO₂ causes cerebral vasoconstriction
  • RV failure patients: May need lower PEEP and higher minute ventilation

Targets:

  • Right arm PaO₂: >8-10 kPa
  • PaCO₂: 4.5-5.5 kPa (ECMO post-oxy and right arm arterial line)
  • pH: >7.35

Circulation

Goal: Balance reversal of shock, maintaining native cardiac output, and optimizing LV unloading

ECMO Settings:

  • Blood flow: Start 3-4 L/min, then titrate
    • Target shock indices (lactate, BE, pH, SvO₂)
    • Minimize flow to reduce afterload (typically 2-3 L/min once stable)
    • Avoid excessive flow causing LV distention
  • Sweep gas flow: Start 3 L/min, titrate to PaCO₂ 4.5-5.5 kPa
  • FdO₂ (sweep gas FiO₂): Target post-oxy PaO₂ 12-15 kPa

Haemodynamic Targets:

  • MAP: 50-55 mmHg (to minimize afterload while maintaining organ perfusion)
    • Consider higher MAP (65-70 mmHg) if refractory shock or Harlequin syndrome
  • Heart rate: 60-100 bpm, sinus rhythm preferred
  • Arterial pulsatility: Pulse pressure >10 mmHg
    • Indicates native cardiac output
    • Loss of pulsatility increases thrombus risk

Inotropic/Vasoactive Support:

  • Low-dose inotrope: Start once ECMO flows established
    • Adrenaline 0.05-0.10 mcg/kg/min, OR
    • Milrinone 0.1-0.3 mcg/kg/min
  • Goal: Maintain aortic valve opening and arterial pulsatility (pulse pressure >10 mmHg)
  • Vasopressors: Noradrenaline titrated to MAP target (minimize dose)
  • Avoid beta-blockers (impair native cardiac output)

Shock Reversal Targets:

  • Lactate: <2.0 mmol/L (gradual correction acceptable)
  • Base excess: >−5.0 mmol/L
  • pH: >7.35
  • SvO₂: >60%
  • Urine output: >0.5 ml/kg/hr
  • Reducing bilirubin and ALT (hepatic perfusion improving)
  • Cerebral NIRS: >55-60% (trending more important than absolute)
Important: The absence of electrical activity or pulse pressure in the first few hours after VA-ECMO (especially E-CPR) does NOT necessarily indicate poor prognosis. Allow time for myocardial recovery.

Organ System Management

Renal

  • Goal: Optimize urine output and fluid balance
  • Strategy:
    • If adequate native renal function → allow to continue
    • Low threshold for RRT initiation (don't manipulate vasoactives/ECMO flows excessively to preserve renal function)
  • Indications for RRT:
    • Anuria/oliguria despite adequate perfusion
    • Fluid overload requiring removal
    • Metabolic acidosis refractory to treatment
    • Hyperkalaemia
    • Uraemia

Neurological

Sedation Strategy:

  • Awake patients: Provide adequate anxiolysis
    • Reduce myocardial work and SVR
    • Low-dose opioids, benzodiazepines, or alpha-2 agonists
    • May require invasive ventilation if significant sedation needed
  • Ventilated patients: Target RASS −2 to −5 for first 24-48 hours
    • Vasoactive-sparing agents preferred: midazolam and fentanyl
    • Low threshold for paralysis infusions during stabilization

Neuroprotection:

  • Temperature management:
    • E-CPR: 35°C for first 24-48 hours, then 36-36.5°C
    • Non-E-CPR: 36-36.5°C throughout
  • Cerebral NIRS monitoring: Target >55-60%
  • Avoid hypotension and hypoxia
  • PaCO₂ target 4.5-5.5 kPa: Avoid hyperventilation (causes cerebral vasoconstriction)

Gastrointestinal

  • NG tube: Insert early
    • Ensure delivery of antiplatelet therapy
    • Gastric decompression
  • Medications: PPI, laxatives (standard)
  • Nutrition:
    • Start enteral feeding slowly once shock indices correcting
    • Low threshold for trophic enteral + parenteral nutrition (gut often ischaemic/shocked)
    • Early dietician referral

Haematology and Anticoagulation

Pre-Anticoagulation Safety Checks:

Before starting heparin infusion, ensure:

  • No significant peripheral bleeding from line sites
  • CT imaging excludes intracranial or chest/abdominal bleeding (if stable enough for CT)

Anticoagulation Strategy:

  • Start unfractionated heparin (UFH) infusion once safety confirmed
    • Use ECMO anticoagulation protocol
    • Target Anti-Xa 0.3-0.5
  • Monitoring:
    • Anti-Xa levels twice daily minimum
    • Daily clotting screen including fibrinogen
    • Ensure valid crossmatch sample at all times

Blood Product Targets:

  • Haemoglobin: >80-90 g/L
  • Platelets: >50 × 10⁹/L (>75 if bleeding)
  • Fibrinogen: >1.5 g/L (>2.0 if bleeding)
  • INR: <1.3 if bleeding

Antiplatelet Therapy:

Many patients require antiplatelet agents for cardiac pathology:

  • Post-ACS/PCI: Aspirin ± P2Y12 inhibitor (clopidogrel/ticagrelor)
  • Route: Via NG tube (ensure absorption confirmed)
  • Balance: Thrombosis risk vs bleeding risk (individualized)

Managing Significant Bleeding:

If major bleeding occurs:

  • TEG for rapid identification of coagulopathy
  • Target: Fibrinogen >2.0 g/L, platelets >75, INR <1.3
  • Stop heparin and consider partial reversal with protamine (max ¼ dose - risk of circuit thrombosis)
  • Tranexamic acid (TXA) infusion:
    • Loading: 12.5 mg/kg over 30 minutes
    • Maintenance: 6.5 mg/kg/hr until bleeding controlled (max 5g total)
    • Main risk: Thrombosis including circuit thrombosis
  • Local haemostasis: Interventional radiology, surgical exploration
  • Factor VIIa: Last resort after haematology consultation

Infection

  • Temperature control: Use ECMO heater-cooler ± surface cooling devices
  • Antibiotics:
    • Already given at cannulation: Teicoplanin + Gentamicin (unless allergic)
    • Low threshold for broad-spectrum antibiotics
    • Daily review and de-escalation as appropriate

Daily Assessments

Clinical Review

Daily multidisciplinary team involvement:

  • ECMO consultant
  • ICU consultant
  • Anaesthesia consultant
  • Cardiology consultant
  • ECMO Nurse Specialist (ENS)
  • Perfusionist
  • Echocardiographer
  • Physiotherapist

Daily Investigations

  • Arterial blood gases:
    • Right arm arterial line (at least twice daily)
    • ECMO post-oxygenator (at least twice daily)
    • Compare for Harlequin syndrome
  • Bloods:
    • FBC, U&E, LFT, bone profile
    • Coagulation screen including fibrinogen
    • CRP, lactate
    • Anti-Xa level (twice daily)
  • Microbiology: Cultures if pyrexial or signs of infection
  • Chest X-ray: Daily (assess pulmonary oedema, line positions, pneumothorax)
  • Echocardiography: Daily minimum (more frequently if LV distention concerns)

Daily Review Points

  1. Shock indices: Trending towards normal? (lactate, BE, SvO₂, urine output)
  2. LV function: Evidence of recovery? Distention? Adequate unloading?
  3. Native cardiac output: Adequate pulsatility? Aortic valve opening?
  4. Complications: Bleeding? Limb ischaemia? Harlequin syndrome?
  5. Weaning potential: Improving towards weaning study or need for transfer?
  6. Destination planning: Stay in Bristol or transfer to RBH/HH?

Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Pitfall Consequence Prevention
No right arm arterial line Missed Harlequin syndrome, cerebral/coronary hypoxia ALWAYS insert right radial/brachial line
No DPC inserted Limb ischaemia, compartment syndrome, amputation Universal DPC at cannulation or within 2-4 hours
Delayed recognition of LV distention Pulmonary oedema, poor cardiac recovery, arrhythmias, thrombus Daily echo, low threshold for LV vent, start inotropes early
Excessive ECMO flows Increased afterload, LV distention, reduced native output Minimize flow once shock resolved (typically 2-3 L/min)
Hyperventilation (low PaCO₂) Cerebral vasoconstriction, reduced cerebral perfusion Target PaCO₂ 4.5-5.5 kPa, avoid excessive MV or sweep gas
Poor native lung ventilation Harlequin syndrome when cardiac function recovers Lung-protective ventilation, optimize FiO₂/PEEP, diurese
Starting anticoagulation too early Catastrophic bleeding (intracranial, thoracic, abdominal) CT imaging first, ensure haemostasis, then start heparin
No NIRS monitoring Delayed detection of cerebral hypoxia or limb ischaemia 4-sensor NIRS standard (bilateral cerebral + bilateral limb)

Key Points Summary

  • Right arm arterial line mandatory: Essential for detecting Harlequin syndrome
  • Universal DPC: Insert at cannulation or within 2-4 hours in all peripheral VA-ECMO
  • Daily echocardiography: Assess LV distention, cardiac recovery, aortic valve opening
  • Start low-dose inotrope early: Maintain pulsatility (pulse pressure >10 mmHg)
  • Minimize ECMO flow: Lowest compatible with shock reversal (typically 2-3 L/min once stable)
  • Target MAP 50-55 mmHg: Reduce afterload, optimize LV unloading
  • Lung-protective ventilation: Prevent Harlequin syndrome as cardiac function recovers
  • PaCO₂ 4.5-5.5 kPa: Avoid hyperventilation (cerebral vasoconstriction)
  • Safety before anticoagulation: CT imaging, ensure haemostasis, then start heparin
  • 4-sensor NIRS: Bilateral cerebral + bilateral limb (standard monitoring)
  • Low threshold for RRT: Don't manipulate ECMO/vasoactives excessively for renal function
  • MDT approach: Daily review by ECMO, ICU, cardiology, perfusion, ENS

Early Referrals

  • Palliative care: At start of next business day
  • Local heart failure service
  • Regional advanced heart failure centre (RBH/HH): Early liaison for destination planning
