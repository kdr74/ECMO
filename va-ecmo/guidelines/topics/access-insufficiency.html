Access Insufficiency

Recognition and Management of Inadequate Venous Drainage

Overview

Especially with smaller calibre venous access used during E-CPR, venous access insufficiency may be a common issue. V-A ECMO flows targeted at shock indices normalisation and LV unloading, rather than total cardiac output for BSA, will limit the flows required. However, if inadequate flows are being provided, then venous insufficiency will need to be addressed.

Clinical Impact: Access insufficiency limits ECMO flow delivery, compromising shock reversal and LV unloading strategies.

Recognition

Standard procedures for recognition include:

  • Chattering lines: Visible pulsation or collapse of venous drainage line
  • Negative venous access pressures: <-100mmHg with low ECMO flows
  • Inability to achieve target flows: Despite adequate pump speed
  • Circuit alarms: Persistent low flow or access pressure alarms

Management Strategies

Initial Assessment

  • Fluid status: Assess and optimize preload
  • Intrathoracic pressure: Review ventilator settings (high PEEP can impede venous return)
  • Cannula position: Verify optimal placement with imaging (echo/fluoroscopy)
  • Cannula kinking: Check for mechanical obstruction

Advanced Interventions

  1. Cannula repositioning: Use imaging guidance to optimize tip position
  2. Additional venous cannula: Insert second drainage cannula (IJ or contralateral femoral)
  3. VV-A ECMO configuration: Convert to dual venous drainage with single arterial return
  4. Larger cannula: Consider upsizing if vessel anatomy permits
VV-A Configuration: Using two venous drainage cannulae connected via Y-connector can significantly improve drainage capacity while maintaining arterial support.

Prevention

  • Appropriate cannula sizing: Select largest feasible venous cannula for patient anatomy
  • Optimal positioning: Ensure bi-caval drainage with tip in RA/SVC junction
  • Early recognition: Monitor venous access pressures continuously
  • Flow targets: Set realistic flow goals based on cannula size and patient BSA
