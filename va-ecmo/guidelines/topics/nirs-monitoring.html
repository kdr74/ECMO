Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (NIRS) Monitoring

Cerebral and Peripheral Tissue Oxygenation on VA-ECMO

Overview

Near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) is a non-invasive monitoring technique that measures regional tissue oxygenation (rSO₂). In VA-ECMO, NIRS provides continuous real-time monitoring of both cerebral and peripheral perfusion, enabling early detection of hypoxia before irreversible damage occurs.

Clinical Applications on VA-ECMO

  • Cerebral monitoring: Detect Harlequin syndrome, cerebral hypoperfusion, stroke
  • Limb monitoring: Early detection of limb ischaemia from arterial cannulation
  • Trending tool: Dynamic changes more important than absolute values
Standard of Care: NIRS monitoring is recommended for all patients on VA-ECMO. A minimum 4-sensor system is required (bilateral cerebral + bilateral lower limb).

NIRS Technology Principles

How NIRS Works

  • Near-infrared light (700-1000 nm wavelength) penetrates tissue to depth of 2-3 cm
  • Measures oxyhaemoglobin and deoxyhaemoglobin concentrations
  • Calculates regional oxygen saturation (rSO₂) as percentage
  • Reflects balance between oxygen delivery and consumption in tissue bed

What NIRS Measures

  • Mixed venous-arterial saturation: Approximately 70% venous, 30% arterial
  • Regional, not global: Measures specific tissue bed under sensor
  • Trend indicator: Changes over time more valuable than single values

Advantages

  • Non-invasive, continuous monitoring
  • Real-time display of tissue oxygenation
  • Early warning of hypoxia (before blood gas changes)
  • Useful in sedated/paralyzed patients (no clinical exam possible)
  • Can detect regional differences (e.g., Harlequin syndrome)

Limitations

  • Absolute values vary: Normal ranges differ between individuals and devices
  • Influenced by skin/subcutaneous tissue: Oedema, dark skin, thick adipose tissue affect readings
  • No standardization: Different manufacturers use different algorithms
  • Cannot distinguish causes: Low rSO₂ could be from many mechanisms
  • Interference: Ambient light, motion artefact, diathermy

Sensor Placement on VA-ECMO

Required Monitoring Sites (4-Sensor Minimum)

1. Bilateral Cerebral NIRS

  • Location: Bilateral forehead, avoiding midline (sagittal sinus)
  • Position: 2-3 cm above eyebrow, avoiding frontal sinuses
  • Purpose:
    • Detect Harlequin syndrome (differential hypoxia)
    • Monitor cerebral perfusion adequacy
    • Identify unilateral events (stroke, arterial occlusion)

2. Bilateral Lower Limb NIRS

  • Location: Both calves (gastrocnemius muscle)
  • Position: Medial or lateral aspect of mid-calf
  • Purpose:
    • Early detection of limb ischaemia (cannula-side limb)
    • Comparison with contralateral limb (control)
    • Monitor distal perfusion cannula effectiveness

Sensor Application Tips

  • Skin preparation: Clean, dry skin; remove excess hair if needed
  • Secure adhesion: Ensure good contact; may need additional tape
  • Avoid pressure: Do not place under tight dressings or equipment
  • Shield from light: Cover sensors with opaque material if in bright environment
  • Document position: Record sensor locations in notes

Normal Values and Thresholds

Normal Ranges

Site Normal Range Concern Threshold
Cerebral rSO₂ 60-75% <55-60%
Lower limb rSO₂ 60-75% <50%

Actionable Thresholds

Cerebral NIRS:

  • Absolute value <55-60%: Investigate cause, optimize perfusion
  • Drop >20% from baseline: Significant change requiring intervention
  • Interhemispheric difference >10%: May indicate unilateral pathology (stroke, arterial stenosis)

Lower Limb NIRS:

  • Absolute value <50%: Significant limb ischaemia
  • Drop >15% from baseline: Investigate distal perfusion
  • Difference >15% between limbs: Ipsilateral limb ischaemia likely
Important: Trends and relative changes are more important than absolute values. A patient with baseline cerebral rSO₂ of 70% dropping to 55% requires investigation, even though 55% may be "normal" for another patient.

Interpreting NIRS Changes

Low Cerebral NIRS (Bilateral)

Potential Causes:

  • Global cerebral hypoperfusion: Low ECMO flow, hypotension, low cardiac output
  • Severe Harlequin syndrome: Both hemispheres perfused by hypoxic native cardiac output
  • Anaemia: Low haemoglobin reduces oxygen carrying capacity
  • Increased metabolic demand: Fever, seizures, agitation, pain
  • Hyperventilation: Low PaCO₂ causes cerebral vasoconstriction

Assessment Steps:

  1. Check sensor placement and quality: Ensure good contact, no artefact
  2. Verify ECMO flows: Adequate pump flow (typically ≥3 L/min)
  3. Check blood pressure: MAP ≥65 mmHg target
  4. Arterial blood gas: From right radial/brachial artery
    • Check PaO₂ (target >10 kPa)
    • Check PaCO₂ (target 4.5-5.5 kPa, avoid <4.0 kPa)
    • Check Hb (target >80 g/L)
  5. Review ventilator settings: Check minute ventilation, avoid hyperventilation
  6. Assess metabolic demand: Treat fever, pain, agitation, seizures

Low Cerebral NIRS (Unilateral)

Potential Causes:

  • Stroke: Ischaemic or haemorrhagic
  • Carotid artery occlusion/stenosis
  • Unilateral sensor malposition
  • Subdural haematoma
  • Asymmetric Circle of Willis (anatomical variant)

Management:

  • Urgent neuroimaging (CT/MRI brain)
  • Neurology consultation
  • Check for focal neurological signs (if awake)
  • Optimize cerebral perfusion pressure

Low Lower Limb NIRS (Unilateral - Cannula Side)

Indicates Limb Ischaemia - See Distal Limb Perfusion guideline

Immediate Actions:

  • Clinical assessment of limb (5 Ps: pain, pallor, pulselessness, paraesthesia, paralysis)
  • Check distal perfusion cannula (if present): patency, connection, flow
  • Optimize systemic perfusion (ECMO flow, MAP)
  • Notify ECMO consultant
  • Insert DPC if not already present (urgent/emergency)

Management Algorithm for Low Cerebral NIRS

Step-by-Step Approach:

Step 1: Verify reading is accurate

  • Check sensor position and adherence
  • Look for signal quality indicators on monitor
  • Consider clinical context (does it match clinical picture?)

Step 2: Optimize oxygen delivery

  • Increase ECMO flow: If <3-4 L/min, consider increasing
  • Increase FiO₂:
    • ECMO sweep gas FiO₂ → aim for post-oxy PaO₂ 12-15 kPa
    • Ventilator FiO₂ → especially if Harlequin syndrome suspected
  • Optimize blood pressure: MAP ≥65 mmHg (consider higher targets 70-80 mmHg)
  • Optimize haemoglobin: Transfuse if Hb <80 g/L (consider higher threshold 90-100 g/L)

Step 3: Reduce oxygen consumption

  • Treat fever: Paracetamol, cooling measures, treat infection
  • Adequate sedation/analgesia: Reduce metabolic demand, prevent agitation
  • Treat seizures: If suspected, consider EEG, anti-epileptic drugs
  • Avoid shivering: Ensure adequate sedation if patient cooled

Step 4: Avoid cerebral vasoconstriction

  • Check PaCO₂: Target 4.5-5.5 kPa
    • If <4.0 kPa (hyperventilation): Reduce minute ventilation and/or ECMO sweep gas flow
    • PaCO₂ is a potent cerebral vasodilator; low levels dramatically reduce cerebral blood flow

Step 5: Consider Harlequin syndrome

  • Compare right arm arterial blood gas with femoral/lower limb gas
  • If significant difference (PaO₂ >3-4 kPa), see Harlequin Syndrome guideline
  • Optimize native lung function, adjust ECMO configuration as needed

Step 6: Investigate other causes

  • Unilateral low NIRS: Consider stroke, urgent CT brain
  • Bilateral persistent low NIRS despite optimization: Consider:
    • CT brain (rule out haemorrhage, ischaemia)
    • Review ECMO circuit (check for air, thrombus, malfunction)
    • Consider increased intracranial pressure (if appropriate clinical context)

Documentation and Communication

What to Document

  • Baseline values: Record initial rSO₂ values for all sensors
  • Sensor positions: Document exact locations
  • Trends: Note any significant changes (>10-20% drops)
  • Interventions: Record actions taken in response to changes
  • Response: Document if interventions improved rSO₂

When to Escalate

Notify ECMO consultant/senior clinician immediately for:

  • Cerebral rSO₂ <55% or drop >20% from baseline
  • Unilateral cerebral NIRS asymmetry >10%
  • Lower limb rSO₂ <50% or drop >15% from baseline
  • Persistent low NIRS despite optimization attempts
  • Clinical signs accompanying low NIRS (altered consciousness, limb changes)

Troubleshooting NIRS Monitoring

Common Problems

Problem Possible Cause Solution
No signal Poor contact, sensor detached Reposition sensor, ensure adhesion, check cable
Low signal quality Ambient light, motion artefact Shield sensor from light, secure position
Erratic readings Electrical interference Check for diathermy, move cables away from ECMO pump
Persistently low values Severe oedema, thick subcutaneous tissue Try alternative site, interpret with caution
Values don't match clinical picture Device malfunction, unusual anatomy Correlate with ABG, clinical exam; consider replacing sensor

Key Points Summary

  • Standard monitoring: All VA-ECMO patients should have 4-sensor NIRS (bilateral cerebral + bilateral lower limb)
  • Trends over absolutes: Changes from baseline are more important than single values
  • Action thresholds: Cerebral <55-60%, limb <50%, or drops >15-20% from baseline
  • Systematic approach: Check sensor → optimize delivery (flow, pressure, Hb, FiO₂) → reduce consumption (fever, pain, seizures) → avoid vasoconstriction (PaCO₂)
  • Harlequin syndrome: Bilateral low cerebral NIRS with good lower body NIRS suggests differential hypoxia
  • Limb ischaemia: Unilateral low limb NIRS requires urgent assessment and DPC insertion
  • Adjunct tool: NIRS complements but does not replace arterial blood gases and clinical examination
  • Early warning system: Allows intervention before irreversible tissue damage occurs
