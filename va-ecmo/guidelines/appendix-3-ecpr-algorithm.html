Appendix 3: BRI / BHI E-CPR Activation Algorithm

Quick reference for extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation activation

Time-Critical Intervention: E-CPR requires rapid decision-making and team activation. This algorithm provides a structured approach to screening and activation for refractory cardiac arrest.

E-CPR Activation Algorithm

BRI / BHI eCPR Activation Algorithm

VF, VT, PEA WITH >3 CYCLES OF ALS

Do NOT proceed if ANY of the following are present:

Key Points for E-CPR Activation

Inclusion Criterion

  • Refractory cardiac arrest with failure to achieve ROSC after 3 cycles of ALS
  • Shockable rhythm (VF, VT) or PEA

Critical Time Windows

  • No flow time: Period without CPR - must be <10 minutes
  • Low flow time: From arrest to ECLS - predicted to be <60 minutes
  • Total arrest time: Minimize as much as possible - outcomes worsen significantly after 60 minutes

Clinical STOP Criteria (ANY excludes E-CPR)

  • Unwitnessed arrest
  • First rhythm asystole
  • No flow time >10 minutes
  • Predicted low flow time >60 minutes
  • Terminal illness
  • Severe neurological impairment
  • Non-cardiac/pulmonary cause of arrest
  • Severe co-morbidities
  • Technical inability to cannulate (vascular disease, body habitus)

Physiological STOP Criteria (≥2 excludes E-CPR)

  • Lactate >18 mmol/L: Severe metabolic failure
  • SpO₂ <85% or PaO₂ <6.6 kPa: Severe refractory hypoxia
  • EtCO₂ <1.3 kPa: Very poor cardiac output/pulmonary blood flow during CPR

Note: These criteria are assessed DURING the ECMO consultant assessment after the initial call.

Activation Process Details

Step 1: Rapid Clinical Assessment (During Ongoing ALS)

  • Gather patient history from notes rapidly
  • Check for clinical STOP criteria
  • If none present, make the E-CPR call

Step 2: Making the E-CPR Call

  1. Dial 2222 (or ICU Outreach Registrar Ext 27139)
  2. State clearly: "Emergency call to Adult ECMO consultant"
  3. When connected, begin with: "This is an E-CPR call"
  4. Provide brief summary:
    • Patient age and location
    • Witnessed arrest? Initial rhythm?
    • Approximate no-flow and low-flow times
    • Known medical history
    • Current rhythm and response to ALS

Step 3: During ECMO Consultant Assessment

  • Attach LUCAS device if not already in place
  • Obtain arterial blood gas for physiological criteria assessment
  • Continue high-quality CPR
  • Follow ECMO consultant instructions for team activation and patient location

Step 4: Physiological Assessment

The ECMO consultant will assess physiological STOP criteria based on:

  • ABG results (lactate, pH, PaO₂)
  • EtCO₂ monitoring
  • SpO₂ values

Decision: If <2 physiological criteria met → proceed with E-CPR

Step 5: E-CPR Team Activation

If decision is to proceed, the Bristol ECMO consultant will coordinate:

  • ECMO team activation (perfusion, ENS, cannulators)
  • Patient location (ideally move to cath lab if stable enough with LUCAS)
  • Equipment preparation
  • Liaison with 2nd decision maker (RBH ECMO consultant or HH ICU consultant)

Important Considerations

Neurological Outcome Focus

E-CPR aims for survival with good neurological outcome. The STOP criteria are designed to exclude patients where:

  • Prolonged hypoxic brain injury is likely (unwitnessed, prolonged no-flow)
  • Severe metabolic derangement suggests multi-organ failure
  • Poor physiological parameters indicate futility

Dual Consultant Decision

E-CPR requires approval from:

  1. Bristol ECMO consultant (primary decision maker)
  2. Second decision maker:
    • Local ECMO mentor, OR
    • RBH ECMO consultant, OR
    • HH ICU consultant

Rapid telephone discussion between both is required even in time-critical situations.

Evidence Base

These criteria are based on contemporary E-CPR evidence including:

  • ARREST trial (Yannopoulos et al, Lancet 2020)
  • Prague OHCA study (Belohlavek et al, EClinicalMedicine 2023)
  • ELSO guidelines for E-CPR (Richardson et al, ASAIO Journal 2021)
