Section 6: Weaning and Transfer within the CS Network

Decision pathways and weaning protocols for VA-ECMO

Local Commissioning Note: Whilst awaiting commissioning, all patients (excluding post-cardiotomy patients) will be transferred to RBH/HH once stability has been achieved. Following commissioning, the protocols below apply.

Transfer Decision Framework

The patient's cardiac pathology and acute physiological needs will determine which site they are managed on. Generally, patients should be evaluated for cardiac recovery on the Bristol site for up to 7 days.

Pathologies Managed Primarily on Bristol Site

May be managed for their entirety on the Bristol site, even if >7 days:

  • Cardiotoxic overdose
  • Myocarditis
  • Septic cardiomyopathy

Pathologies Requiring Early Transfer to RBH/HH

Need to be transferred to RBH/HH immediately after stability achieved:

  • Chronic dilated cardiomyopathy
  • Heart transplant recipients with rejection

Close liaison between the Bristol and RBH/HH ECMO and Cardiology teams is required to determine transfer requirements.

Standard Practice Decision Pathways

1. Recovery of Native Cardiac Function ("Bridge to Recovery")

Scenario A: Recovery started in 5-7 days and likely to be sustained

  • Action: Patient will remain on the Bristol site and be weaned locally

Scenario B: Recovery apparent by 5-7 days but likely to be complex, OR initial recovery has secondary deterioration

  • Action: Patient should be transferred to the advanced heart failure centre (RBH/HH)

2. No Recovery - Suitable for Escalation ("Bridge to Destination Therapy")

No recovery of native cardiac function → patient suitable for escalation to durable device therapy / heart transplant

  • Action: Patient should be transferred to the advanced heart failure centre (RBH/HH) for destination therapy assessment

3. No Recovery - Unsuitable for Escalation ("Bridge to Decision")

No recovery of native cardiac function → patient unsuitable for escalation to durable device therapy / heart transplant

Decision: A decision will be made by the combined Bristol and HH/RBH MDT regarding:

  • Continued MCS: Patient should be transferred to the advanced heart failure centre (RBH/HH)
  • WLST (Withdrawal of Life Sustaining Treatments): Patient should remain on the Bristol site and have palliative care performed locally

Transfer Arrangements

Transfer to the advanced heart failure centre (RBH/HH) will be undertaken by the Bristol ECMO team, with exact details decided case by case.

Weaning Protocol

The weaning process consists of reducing the amount of ECMO support while closely monitoring the patient's haemodynamics and echocardiographic measurements. This reduction in ECMO support leads to changes in the loading conditions of the heart, which includes an increase in preload of the heart and a decrease in systemic afterload. When the heart is ready to be weaned off ECMO, the cardiac output will remain stable or even augment, without developing any new signs of heart failure.

Decision to Wean Locally

If a decision is made to wean the patient locally in Bristol, follow the below steps:

Pre-requisites Prior to Weaning

  • Bristol and RBH/HH ECMO and cardiology teams agree on the weaning study
  • Underlying pathology has had sufficient time to recover
  • No shock confounders present: Infection, bleeding, hypervolemia or tamponade

Haemodynamic Stability Requirements

  • Stable ECMO flow (ideally <3 L/min) and on <3 L/min fresh gas flow
  • Consistent arterial pulsatility >24 hours and Pulse pressure >20 mmHg
  • MAP >60 mmHg in the absence or with low doses of inotrope/vasopressors
  • Vasopressor dosing: Noradrenaline <0.10 mcg/kg/min
  • Inotrope dosing: Adrenaline <0.05 mcg/kg/min, milrinone <0.25 mcg/kg/min, dobutamine <5 mcg/kg/min
  • Stable cardiac rhythm (preferably sinus rhythm)
  • Stable metabolic state: Lactate <2.0, pH >7.35, SvO₂ >60%

Pulmonary Function Requirements

  • Minimal pulmonary infiltrates on chest X-ray
  • P/F ratio >13.3 with ventilator FiO₂ <60% and ECMO FGF set to FiO₂ 21%
Alternative to Full Decannulation: Consider reconfiguration to VV-ECMO rather than full decannulation if other pre-requisites to weaning are met, but P/F ratio <13.3 or on >60% FiO₂.

Other Requirements

  • No evolving organ dysfunction: May continue on RRT

Echocardiographic Requirements

Formal echocardiography examination suggestive of improvement of cardiac function:

  • LV normal size (LVIDd and LVEDV)
  • LV EF >20-25%
  • LVOT VTi >10cm
  • MV S' (lateral) >6cm/s
  • RV normal size (RVD2 most accurate)
  • RV function improving (no absolute figures for TAPSE / RV S' / FAC)
  • Aortic valve opening with every cardiac contraction
  • Absence of excessive "smoke" (SEC) in the ventricle
  • Absence of a significant pericardial effusion / collection

Weaning Study Procedure

If all pre-requisites are met, the patient should undergo a weaning study. This takes place on ICU, with the ECMO consultant, echocardiography trained personnel (TTE or TOE) and ENS.

Documentation

Haemodynamics, vasoactive dosing and echocardiographic values should be monitored at baseline (i.e. prior to a reduction in ECMO support) and at each level of reduced ECMO flow. These parameters should be documented on the standardised ECMO weaning form (Appendix 5).

Inotrope Preparation

If not already instigated, it may be appropriate to commence inotropes in anticipation of a weaning study.

Anticoagulation During Weaning

The heparin infusion should be continued for the weaning study and during decannulation, a fundamental difference to the V-V ECMO pathway.

Weaning Steps

  1. Reduce ECMO flow by 1 L/min and assess for 5-10 minutes at each new flow rate
  2. If stability is maintained at each level, continue to reduce until at 1 L/min
  3. At 1 L/min, perform final assessment (e.g. 3 → 2 → 1 L/min)
  4. Extended periods (i.e. more than 5 minutes) at 1 L/min should be avoided except in exceptional circumstances
  5. At 1 L/min, only the LVEF, LVOT VTI & MV S' (lateral) on echocardiography need to be assessed

Pump-Controlled Retrograde Trial Off (PCRTO)

If the above conventional wean is considered borderline – perform PCRTO to better evaluate a fully loaded cardiovascular system (See Appendix 5).

PCRTO Procedure

Used to further load the cardiovascular system when conventional wean results are borderline:

  • Sweep gas flow is switched off
  • Flow probe is reversed
  • ECMO console revs are reduced
  • Allows small amount of retrograde pump flow (1-1.5 L/min) to be achieved and measured
  • Provides fuller loading of cardiovascular system without risk of ECMO circuit clotting

Weaning Study with Impella

If an Impella is in situ, the weaning study should include the same ECMO weaning steps, but at each ECMO weaning step different Impella P levels should also be trialled, with haemodynamic variables recorded. This is especially important at the lower ECMO flows (1L/min) and during PCRTO when weaning success is uncertain - to see if weaning is possible with increased Impella support.

Definition of Successful V-A ECMO Weaning

There is no single parameter that predicts a successful wean, always use a combination of qualitative and quantitative measures to help predict whether a patient can safely be liberated from V-A ECMO.

Parameters Associated with Likely Success

The following parameters are associated with a likely success, especially when seen with PCRTO retrograde flows:

No Significant Change in Haemodynamic Parameters

  • A fall in MAP below 55 mmHg or a fall of greater than 20 mmHg with ECMO blood flow reduction is considered clinically significant
  • A rise in CVP (or MPAP – if predominately LVF) of greater than 5 mmHg or to a value greater than 15 mmHg (35 mmHg for MPAP) is considered significant

No Significant Increase in Vasoactives

  • Inotrope doses prior to the weaning study should be set to levels expected to be required following decannulation
  • A rise of noradrenaline for hypotension (see above) of more than 0.05 mcg/kg/min during the weaning phase is considered significant

At Minimum ECMO Flow Support or During PCRTO:

  • LVEF >20-25%
  • LVOT VTI ≥10 cm
  • MV S' (lateral) ≥6 cm/s
  • Recruitment of LV and / or RV function (qualitatively or quantitatively)
  • Absence of significant LV dilatation
  • No abnormal decrease of the LV dimensions in the presence of RV dilation
  • No right ventricular failure
  • No cardiac tamponade

Decannulation

If the weaning study is successful, decannulation should occur. This will occur in cardiac theatre with the full theatre team, ECMO consultant, cardiac anaesthesia, echocardiography trained personnel (TTE or TOE), vascular-competent surgeon, perfusion and ENS. The cardiac surgeons should be contacted to assist if vascular surgery is not available.

Timing

  • The theatre case for decannulation should be booked after a successful weaning study has been completed
  • There should be no longer than 24hrs between successful weaning study and decannulation procedure
  • Partial ECMO support (no lower than 2.0 L/min ECMO flows) will need to be re-instituted until decannulation occurs
  • Anticoagulation should continue
  • A mini weaning study may be performed in theatre prior to decannulation if there has been a significant time interval between the previous weaning study and accessing theatre

Cannulae Removal

Decannulation will involve removal of both the venous and arterial cannulae, as well as the distal perfusion cannula (DPC).

Exception: When a staged wean to V-V ECMO is planned, where the current venous access cannula will be retained, a further venous return will be inserted and the arterial cannula and DPC removed.

Surgical Technique

  • Venous cannula removal: Requires superficial sutures and pressure (see V-V ECMO SOP)
  • Arterial and distal perfusion cannula removal: Requires full surgical control of the femoral vessels, embolectomy and repair as required - by a qualified vascular or cardiac surgeon
  • Anticoagulation: Should continue throughout the decannulation process

Unsuccessful Weaning Study

If the patient is deemed to have failed the weaning study, consideration is given to:

  • Further medical optimisation
  • Requirement for a "stepdown" mechanical support device (Impella CP) if not already in-situ
  • Timing of the next weaning study
  • Requirement for transfer to the advanced heart failure centre (RBH/HH)

This evaluation is performed by the ECMO and cardiology teams locally and in liaison with the advanced heart failure centre (RBH/HH).

Key Points Summary

  • Bristol site evaluation up to 7 days: For suitable pathologies (myocarditis, cardiotoxicity, septic cardiomyopathy)
  • Early transfer for chronic pathologies: Dilated cardiomyopathy, transplant rejection
  • Three destination pathways: Bridge to recovery, bridge to destination therapy, bridge to decision
  • Pre-requisites critical: MDT agreement, haemodynamic stability, adequate pulmonary function, echo evidence of recovery
  • ECMO flows <3 L/min stable >24 hours: Required before weaning study
  • Pulse pressure >20 mmHg: Indicates adequate native cardiac output
  • Echo thresholds: LVEF >20-25%, LVOT VTi >10cm, MV S' >6cm/s
  • Stepwise flow reduction: 1 L/min decrements with 5-10 min assessment at each level
  • PCRTO for borderline cases: Provides fuller cardiovascular loading assessment
  • Continue anticoagulation throughout: Including during weaning study and decannulation
  • Decannulation within 24 hours: Of successful weaning study
  • Vascular surgical closure required: For arterial and DPC removal
  • VV-ECMO conversion option: If cardiac function recovered but poor pulmonary function persists
  • Consider Impella as bridge: For borderline weans or stepdown support
