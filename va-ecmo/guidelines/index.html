Veno-Arterial Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (V-A ECMO)

Clinical Guideline for Adult Patients

Version: V1.8 (December 2024 - December 2026)

Authors: Matt Govier, Stefan Gurney, James Doyle, Alex Rosenberg (ICU/ECMO Consultants), D. Machin (Perfusion Lead)

Setting: University Hospitals Bristol and Weston NHS Foundation Trust

Overview

Veno-Arterial Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (V-A ECMO) is a form of mechanical circulatory support that can be implemented broadly for two patient groups where optimal conventional therapies have failed:

  • Cardiogenic shock with high SCAI grading (SCAI D or E)
  • Refractory cardiac arrest without return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) - termed "E-CPR"
Important: V-A ECMO initiation is a time-sensitive, complex intervention and carries significant mortality and morbidity risk. Selection of appropriate patients is vital and should be guided by experienced members of the MDT, both locally and at the regional advanced heart failure centre (Royal Brompton Hospital / Harefield Hospital).

Target Audience

  • Medical, nursing, perfusion and AHP groups
  • Critical care staff
  • Cardiac anaesthesia teams
  • Cardiology teams
  • Cardiac surgery teams
  • ECMO team members

Patient Population

Selected adult patients in:

  • Cardiogenic shock (SCAI D/E)
  • Cardiac arrest requiring E-CPR
  • Post-cardiotomy cardiogenic shock or arrest

Main Sections

Section 1: Indications

Patient selection criteria for V-A ECMO, including:

  • Cardiogenic shock indications (SCAI D-E criteria)
  • Exclusion criteria
  • Risk stratification (SAVE score, ECMO risk matrix)
  • E-CPR indications and rapid STOP criteria
  • Post-cardiotomy patient specifics

Section 2: Location

Where V-A ECMO can be performed:

  • Cannulation locations (bedside, cath lab, theatre)
  • Cardiac cath lab and CT imaging requirements
  • Ongoing care locations (A600, C604, transfer pathways)
  • Post-cardiotomy patient locations

Section 3: V-A ECMO Initiation Procedure

Step-by-step cannulation and initiation:

  • Personnel and team setup
  • Preparation and imaging options
  • Securing vessels (arterial and venous access)
  • Sequential dilation technique
  • Cannula insertion and sizing
  • Instigating perfusion
  • Distal limb perfusion cannula placement
  • Surgical assistance when needed
  • E-CPR modifications (rapid technique)

Section 4: Immediate Care After Initiating V-A ECMO

Post-cannulation pathway and management:

  • Location pathway (cannulation → cath lab → CT → ICU)
  • Essential interventions (arterial lines, PAC, echo, coronary angiography)
  • Physiological targets (ventilation, circulation, LV unloading)
  • Anticoagulation protocols
  • Bleeding management (including TXA protocol)
  • Temperature control
  • Multidisciplinary care coordination

Section 5: Specific V-A ECMO Complications

Recognition and management of major complications:

  • LV Distention Syndrome: Detection, medical management, mechanical LV venting devices
  • Harlequin Syndrome (Differential Hypoxia): Pathophysiology, detection, management strategies
  • Access Insufficiency: Recognition and management including VV-A ECMO configuration
  • Loss of Arterial Pulsatility: Causes, consequences, assessment, management

Section 6: Weaning and Transfer Within the CS Network

Decision pathways for definitive outcomes:

  • Transfer decision framework (Bridge to Recovery, Bridge to Destination Therapy, Bridge to Decision)
  • 5-7 day assessment period on Bristol site
  • Pre-requisites for weaning studies
  • Weaning study procedure (conventional wean and PCRTO)
  • Success criteria for liberation from VA-ECMO
  • Decannulation procedure
  • Unsuccessful wean management
  • Conversion to V-V ECMO or V-AV ECMO hybrid

Section 7: Post-Cardiotomy Patient Specifics

Special considerations for post-cardiac surgery patients:

  • Indications (intra-operative failure to wean from CPB, post-operative shock/arrest)
  • Early recognition and decision-making (VIS score thresholds)
  • Cannulation approaches (central vs peripheral, wet circuit change from CPB)
  • Anticoagulation timing (delayed heparinization 12-48hrs)
  • Chest closure decisions (open vs closed)
  • LV venting strategies (LA/PV vent for central, Impella/IABP for peripheral)
  • Modified weaning timeframes (48-72hrs vs 5-7 days)
  • Location pathway (CICU C604 → A600/RBH/HH)
  • Perfusion staffing implications

Appendices

Appendix 1: Cardiogenic Shock Network

Regional network structure and patient flow pathways between UHBW Bristol (CS Centre) and RBH/HH (Advanced Heart Failure Centre), including shock call protocols and geographic coverage area.

Appendix 2: SCAI Grading of Cardiogenic Shock

Complete SCAI classification system (Stages A-E) with physical exam findings, biochemical markers, hemodynamics, and observed mortality data. Includes stepwise risk assessment algorithm.

Appendix 3: BRI / BHI E-CPR Activation Algorithm

Quick reference card for E-CPR activation including clinical STOP criteria, call procedure (2222), and physiological STOP criteria assessed during ECMO consultant evaluation.

Appendix 4: Cerebral NIRS Protocol

Comprehensive protocol for cerebral near-infrared spectroscopy monitoring including target values, one-sided drop algorithm, bilateral drop algorithm, and management flowcharts.

Appendix 5: Standardised ECMO Weaning Study Form

Documentation template for weaning assessments with comprehensive data collection fields, PCRTO explanation, and success criteria reminders.
