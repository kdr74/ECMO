Section 2: Location

Where VA-ECMO Procedures Occur and Patient Pathway

Cannulation

General Principles

Instigation of V-A ECMO can occur anywhere on the BRI/BHI site of UHBW if the patient is too unstable to transfer. Cannulations in areas outside of cath labs/theatre will be performed under ultrasound scan (USS) or echocardiography guidance.

If the patient is able to be transferred safely, the procedure may be performed in either a cardiac cath lab or theatre, with fluoroscopy guidance available.

Post-Cardiotomy Patients

Post-cardiotomy patients should be cannulated in the theatre where they have been operated on, if they cannot be weaned from bypass.

Patients Outside BRI/BHI Footprint

Patients outside of the BRI/BHI footprint, either within UHBW or at other local trusts, cannot currently receive instigation of V-A ECMO unless they are able to be transferred to the BRI/BHI site.

These patients should be discussed early with:

  • Local Bristol ECMO consultant
  • RBH/HH ECMO consultant as part of a shock call

A decision will need to be taken regarding:

  • If conventional management is sufficient to allow safe transfer of the patient, OR if an ECMO retrieval team is required to instigate ECMO at the patient's current location
  • The destination hospital – either BRI, or onward to RBH/HH

Cardiac Cath Labs / CT Imaging

If instigation of V-A ECMO occurs outside of the cardiac cath labs, it is highly likely the patient will require transfer to the cardiac cath lab prior to being admitted to ICU.

Indications for Cath Lab Transfer

  • Diagnostic coronary angiogram +/- PCI
  • Insertion of distal perfusion cannula (if not able to be performed bedside)
  • LV venting (Impella or Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump)

CT Imaging

The patient will also require CT imaging of the head, chest and abdomen to rule out early bleeding complications and to further guide diagnostics.

If stable enough, this should occur prior to ICU admission, but may need to be delayed until stability has been achieved.

Standard CT Protocol:

  • CT head: Rule out intracranial haemorrhage (especially post-OHCA)
  • CT neck: If OHCA with intubation
  • CTPA: Assess pulmonary vasculature and parenchyma
  • CT aortogram: Assess aorta, cannula position
  • CT abdomen/pelvis: Rule out bleeding, assess visceral perfusion

Ongoing Care

Pre-Commissioning Pathway

Whilst awaiting commissioning: All patients (excluding post-cardiotomy patients) will be transferred to RBH/HH once stability has been achieved.

Post-Commissioning Pathway

Once commissioned locally: These patients will be cared for in the A600 footprint.

Post-Cardiotomy Patients

Post-cardiotomy patients will be cared for on C604 (CICU) in the initial 48-72 hours, given the high likelihood of:

  • Return to theatre
  • Open chest management
  • Bleeding complications

Decision at 48-72 Hours:

If the requirement for V-A ECMO remains at 48-72 hours, a decision should be made about:

  • Transfer to A600
  • Remaining on C604
  • Transferring to RBH/HH

This decision involves:

  • Local Bristol ECMO consultant
  • CICU consultant
  • Cardiac surgical consultant (initially)
  • Then RBH/HH ECMO and cardiac surgical teams

Location Summary by Patient Type

Patient Type Cannulation Location Ongoing Care Location
Cardiogenic Shock (stable) Cath lab or theatre (with fluoroscopy) A600 (GICU) → transfer to RBH/HH*
Cardiogenic Shock (unstable) Bedside (ICU, ward, ED) with USS/echo A600 (GICU) → transfer to RBH/HH*
E-CPR Site of arrest (with USS/TOE) A600 (GICU) → transfer to RBH/HH*
Post-Cardiotomy Cardiac theatre (central cannulation from CPB) C604 (CICU) for 48-72hrs → then decision

* Pre-commissioning pathway. Post-commissioning: patients may remain at Bristol site for up to 7 days

Key Points Summary

  • Cannulation flexible: Can occur anywhere on BRI/BHI site if patient too unstable to transfer
  • Cath lab preferred: If patient stable enough, allows fluoroscopy guidance
  • Post-cardiotomy in theatre: Cannulate where surgery occurred if can't wean from CPB
  • Early MDT discussion: For patients outside BRI/BHI footprint (retrieval vs transfer)
  • Cath lab post-cannulation: Most patients need coronary angiogram, DPC, LV vent assessment
  • CT imaging essential: Head/neck/chest/abdomen before ICU admission if stable
  • A600 (GICU) standard: For non-cardiotomy patients (post-commissioning)
  • C604 (CICU) for post-cardiotomy: Initial 48-72 hours due to bleeding/reoperation risk
  • Transfer to RBH/HH: Pre-commissioning pathway for all non-cardiotomy patients
