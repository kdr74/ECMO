Section 3: V-A ECMO Initiation Procedure

Cannulation Technique and Team Setup

Overview

Instigation of V-A ECMO for cardiogenic shock and E-CPR, although similar, have important differences. Excellent teamwork to ensure a timely and uncomplicated instigation of this complex support is required.

Key Difference: E-CPR requires modified rapid technique with skipped steps to minimize time to perfusion. Standard cardiogenic shock cannulation uses sequential technique with optimal imaging.

Personnel and Equipment Setup

Regardless of location, the following personnel setup is recommended (adjusted to clinical situation/location as required):

Team Roles

  • Team Leader: Overall coordination, decision-making
  • Airway Doctor: Airway management, drugs, anaesthesia
  • Scribe: Documentation, timing, communication
  • CPR/LUCAS: Chest compressions (E-CPR only)
  • Cannulator 1: Primary vascular access operator
  • Cannulator 2: Secondary vascular access operator
  • Imaging operator: Fluoroscopy/TTE/TOE/USS guidance
  • ENS (ECMO Nurse Specialist): Circuit preparation, equipment
  • Perfusionist: Circuit priming, ECMO console operation

Equipment Requirements

  • Procedure trolley: Sterile drapes, sheaths, dilators, cannulae
  • Imaging: Fluoroscopy OR TTE/TOE OR USS machine
  • ECMO circuit: Primed CardioHelp system
  • ECMO console: With perfusion team

Reference Diagram: See page 7 of main PDF guideline for personnel positioning diagram showing optimal team arrangement around patient.

Cannulation for Cardiogenic Shock

This aligns closely with the SOP "Mobile ECMO cannulation and initiation" used for the V-V ECMO cannulation service. General principles are similar, although specific differences are noted below.

Preparation

  • ENS: Prepare trolley, flush sheaths/dilators/cannulae
  • Cannulator 1 and 2: Full aseptic preparation of groins
  • Cannulator 1 and 2: Cover patient with small and then angiography drapes
  • Cannulator 1 and 2: Apply ultrasound probe sheath, secure to drape with towel clip
  • Perfusionist: Prime ECMO circuit as per SOP "Assembly and Priming of the CardioHelp and commencing ECMO"

Imaging Options for Wire and Cannulae Position

  • For patients cannulated outside cath lab/theatre:
    • USS/TTE with transhepatic/subcostal view (via cannulators or separate operator)
    • TOE (by separate operator)
  • For patients who can transfer to/are already in cath lab or theatre with image intensifier:
    • Fluoroscopy combined with USS/Echocardiography should be used

Securing the Vessels

Puncture of the common femoral artery and vein requires real-time USS imaging. Access (venous) and return (arterial) cannula can be inserted in the same or opposite groins, depending on personnel and anatomy.

Arterial Access - Critical Points

Retrograde cannulation of the common femoral artery should be achieved approximately 2cm caudal to the inguinal ligament, and away from large calcific deposits.

This is to prevent:

  • Cannulation of the profunda or superficial femoral artery
  • Puncture of the external iliac artery with risk of retroperitoneal haemorrhage

Distal Perfusion Cannula Wire Placement (if time allows)

If the patient has relative stability, antegrade insertion of a guidewire into the ipsilateral superficial femoral artery (SFA) can be attempted after insertion of the arterial sheath, but before insertion of the arterial cannula. This will be used to instigate distal limb perfusion.

  • A micropuncture kit is often useful
  • Secure the wire with a towel clip or replace with a 4-6Fr sheath
  • Delay insertion of the antegrade reperfusion cannula until the ECMO drainage and return cannula have been inserted and the patient is established on ECMO

Step-by-Step Vessel Access

Cannulators:

  1. Real time USS of vessels to be cannulated
  2. Access vessel with needle – if small calibre artery, a micro-puncture kit may assist
  3. Insert short wire up into the common femoral artery and confirm position with USS
  4. If time allows – insert wire into ipsilateral SFA, for distal perfusion cannula, as above
  5. Insert 6F angiography sheath retrograde via Seldinger technique. Nick the skin if needed
  6. Remove dilator and wire
  7. Aspirate, flush and lock the sheath – transducing the sheath if required to confirm arterial vs venous location
  8. Repeat above steps to cannulate the common femoral vein

Venous Wire Placement:

  1. Insert 145cm Amplatz extra stiff wire into the venous sheath and carefully advance
  2. Confirm J of wire in RA/SVC (via fluoroscopy, TTE or TOE)

Arterial Wire Placement (Standard Technique):

  1. Load a soft guidewire into MPA, insert into descending aorta through the arterial sheath
  2. Advance the 100cm 6Fr MPA over the soft wire – until wire J is straightened
  3. Remove soft wire, whilst leaving MPA in place, replace with 145cm Amplatz extra stiff wire and advance until J appears above MPA on USS/fluoroscopy
  4. Remove MPA
STOP: If rapid instigation of VA-ECMO is required, do NOT use the above soft wire/MPA technique, but insert the Amplatz extra stiff wire directly through the arterial sheath into the descending aorta.

Drugs/Airway Doctor:

  • Administer systemic bolus of 25-50 iu/kg intravenous unfractionated heparin to achieve a target ACT of 200-220 at time of cannulation
  • Administer IV antibiotics (Teicoplanin / Gentamicin, unless allergic)

Sequential Dilation of Vessels

Start with venous femoral site. Ideally this is a 3-person technique, but can be performed by 2 people if required:

Dilation Sequence

  1. Cannulator 1: Remove 6F sheath with pressure for haemostasis as it is withdrawn over wire
  2. ENS/Scrub Nurse: Removes sheath, feed 10F dilator onto venous wire, hold wire securely
  3. Cannulator 1: Take 10Fr dilator and insert into skin
  4. Cannulator 1: Remove 10F dilator with pressure for haemostasis as it is withdrawn over wire
  5. ENS/Scrub Nurse: Remove 10F dilator, feed 12F dilator onto venous wire first, hold wire securely
  6. Cannulator 1: Take 12Fr dilator and insert into skin
  7. Cannulator 2: Remove sheath from the arterial site in the same way ensuring haemostasis
  8. ENS/Scrub Nurse: Remove sheath, feed 10F dilator onto arterial wire, hold wire securely
  9. Cannulator 2: Take 10Fr dilator and insert into skin
  10. Cannulators 1 and 2: Take the dilators and insert them while ENS/Scrub nurse holds the wire secure. This continues at 2Fr steps, until dilators 1F larger or the same size as the cannulae have been inserted

Recommended Cannula Sizes

Cannula Type Size Max Dilator Vessel Indication
Multistage drainage (venous) 25F 25F Femoral Vein Standard
Multistage drainage (venous) 21F 22F Femoral Vein Smaller patients (<60kg) or E-CPR
Single stage return (arterial) 17F 18F Femoral Artery Standard
Single stage return (arterial) 15F 16F Femoral Artery Smaller patients (<60kg) or E-CPR

Note: The clinical situation may warrant a differing approach to cannula sizing.

Cannula Insertion

ENS/Scrub Nurse

  • Prepares cannulae – flush and insert stylet through rubber cap, secure bungs, remove white "stay" device

Cannulators - Start with Venous Drainage Cannula:

  1. Confirm wire tip remains in the SVC
  2. Remove final dilator ensuring haemostasis
  3. Load cannula onto wire, ensuring wire is seen freely outside distal end of pipe
  4. Drainage cannula will bleed on insertion from drainage sites until all are inserted into the patient; at this stage withdraw stylet to the mark at the bung
  5. Continue to insert the cannulae over the wire with the appropriate guidance
  6. Site cannula tip at the SVC for bi-caval drainage (approx. 55cm)
    • RA position is acceptable, with TOE repositioning if needed later
  7. Remove wire and stylet and clamp cannula below the blood level
  8. Flush cannula with heparinised saline (10,000iu in 1000ml) using bladder syringe and clamp cannula below the fluid level

Cannulators - Arterial Return Cannula:

Repeat the above steps for insertion of return cannula into femoral artery, ensuring:

  • Wire is confirmed in the aorta (with either echo/fluoroscopy)
  • Arterial cannula is pushed into the taper

Surgical Assistance

If at any stage the arterial (+/- venous) vessel access, dilatation or cannulae insertion is unsuccessful or challenging, an urgent call should be made to the on-call cardiothoracic registrar to attend.

It may be decided to continue with attempts to access the vessels percutaneously, as the favoured technique to reduce bleeding complications – but simultaneously preparation should begin for a surgical approach to vessel access.

Surgical Preparation

If surgical access is likely to be required:

  • On-call cardiothoracic registrar should contact the cardiac surgical consultant
  • Activate the cardiac theatre team
  • Source a cardiac surgical tray from cardiac theatres

Hybrid Open-Seldinger Technique

A hybrid open-Seldinger technique, similar to establishing femoral CPB, is used - but with small modifications to help ensure the cannulae remain secured in position without bleeding:

  1. Perform vertical femoral cut down rapidly and identify the vessels (CFA/CFV)
  2. Priority is given to inserting the cannulas. Purse strings, management of bleeding and distal perfusion cannula insertion etc. can be done after starting perfusion
  3. Insertion of cannulae is with the Seldinger technique and preferably tunnelled through the skin a few centimetres inferior to the incision
  4. The original wire that comes with the cannula is initially inserted through the needle, but this wire must be replaced with a stiff wire once the size 8 dilator is in the femoral artery
  5. Use echo guidance to confirm position of the wires initially, and again to confirm the position once the cannulae are inserted
  6. After starting perfusion (see below), purse strings are placed around the cannula insertion site using 5-0 or 4-0 prolene, with half size snuggers used until haemostasis is achieved
  7. A distal perfusion cannula is then inserted
  8. The snuggers are left inside and stabilised by 'peanut swabs'. Size 16 bellow vac drain is inserted at the cut down site, and the subcutaneous layers and skin are closed in a standard fashion using 1 Vicryl and 3-0 monocryl. Alternatively, skin clips can be used for skin

Side Graft Alternative

On occasions, a side graft for the arterial inflow may instead be utilised. This technique increases the risk of bleeding complications, but vastly reduces distal limb ischaemia risks and often negates the requirement for a distal perfusion cannula.

Instigating Perfusion

Cannulators:

  • Prepare ECMO lines under supervision of perfusionist – remove ECMO side of circuit from sash, clamp below the cut tapes and remove from prime bag side by disconnecting at the posilocks. Maintain sterility at all times. Cut lines to appropriate length, ensure they are untangled, and pass to centre of table
  • STOP - Ensure venous line correlates with venous cannula – and similarly arterial side

Cannulators and ENS/Scrub Nurse:

  • Connect circuit to cannulae, using "wet-join" connection technique. Push tubing over first spur, check for air, if no air push tubing over second spur. Repeat for second cannula
  • Remove clamps and go onto ECMO as per "Perfusion" below
  • Dress and secure lines as per the V-V ECMO protocols

Perfusion:

  1. Set the Revolutions per minute (RPM) on the CardioHelp to 1000 and ensure that sweep gas is set to an FiO₂ of 1.0 and a gas flow rate of 1 litre/per minute
  2. The cannulators release their clamps once they have checked the perfusion team have control
  3. The perfusion team release the clamp on the drainage limb and then the return limb
  4. The RPM is then increased to achieve the desired extra-corporeal blood flow rate. This will be 3-4L/min initially, titrated to shock markers and side effects of increased afterload (LV distention, cardiac ejection and R upper body perfusion)
  5. Blood gas analysis should be undertaken from:
    • Right upper body arterial line
    • Pre-oxygenator
    • Post-oxygenator
    ...and ventilator/ECMO settings adjusted accordingly
  6. An ACT should be checked to assess the response to heparin

Distal Limb Perfusion Cannula (DPC)

A short reinforced 6-9Fr armoured sheath should be inserted over an antegrade wire in the SFA, AFTER other ECMO cannulae have been inserted.

Ideally the wire will have been placed into the SFA prior to ECMO cannula insertion. If no armoured sheath is available, a standard angiography sheath (6-8Fr) can be used temporarily.

If technically possible, the DPC should be inserted in the location of ECMO cannulation to expedite instigation of flow down the distal limb.

For full DPC connection technique and monitoring, see Distal Limb Perfusion guideline

E-CPR Specific Modifications

Due to the time-critical nature of E-CPR, technique is similar but modified from that described for cardiogenic shock.

Changes to ALS Algorithm

Once the decision has been made to proceed to E-CPR, the CPR team leader should ensure the following occurs whilst cannulation and perfusion is established:

  • Stop adrenaline boluses
  • Stop all defibrillation
  • Stop all other attempts at vascular access
  • Use LUCAS where possible, to ensure uninterrupted high quality chest compressions
  • Only pause compressions for ECMO needle insertion if absolutely necessary
  • Stop all chest compressions once ECMO flows established

Modified Cannulation Technique for E-CPR

Key Differences:

  • No transducing pressures or sampling gases to establish sheath position (likely misleading)
  • If vessel access achieved with needle, place short sheath wire into vessel and use USS to determine which vessel it is in
  • Do NOT attempt to place a distal perfusion cannula wire
  • Correct position of wires in vena cava and descending aorta performed with TOE, USS or TTE (via transhepatic/subcostal view). Do NOT use fluoroscopy
  • Do NOT use the MPA/Soft wire technique for arterial cannula insertion – go straight to the Amplatz extra-stiff wire through the arterial sheath
  • Do NOT perform sequential dilatation for cannulae insertion. Instead:
    • Once the sheath is removed, make a large incision through the skin by the guidewire
    • Move rapidly through a 10Fr and 16Fr dilator – and then straight to inserting a dilator of 1Fr larger than the venous cannula – hub to the skin
    • Remove the final dilator and progress straight to inserting the cannula
  • Cannulae for E-CPR are 21-25Fr access (venous) and 15Fr return (arterial)
  • Do NOT utilize imaging techniques to optimally locate the venous (access) cannulae position. Insert this to 50-55cm at the skin, commence ECMO flows and adjust position with TOE/TTE guidance after flows are established
  • Flush and clamp the cannula only if starting ECMO flows is likely to be delayed (i.e. single operator inserting both cannulae). Otherwise, clamping cannulae after insertion, with blood remaining in the line and rapid movement to instigating perfusion is preferred

E-CPR Initial ECMO Parameters

  • ECMO blood flow: 3 L/min
  • Sweep gas flow: 3 L/min
  • FdO₂: 100% (14 on dial)

Gases (R arm and post-oxy) should be taken every 15-30 mins post instigation of ECMO, to titrate the EBF/SGF/FdO₂, which may need to be reduced rapidly. Standard PaO₂/PaCO₂ targets as per cardiogenic shock V-A ECMO should be targeted.

E-CPR Anticoagulation

It is recommended to still utilise heparin in the ECMO machine prime and as a small (10-25 units/kg) bolus at the time of cannulae insertion. This is to prevent rapid mechanical failure.

Cannulation Differences Summary: Cardiogenic Shock vs E-CPR

Cannulation Practice Cardiogenic Shock E-CPR
Imaging for wire/cannulae Fluoroscopy + TTE/TOE/USS TTE/TOE/USS - Do NOT use fluoroscopy
DPC wire insertion Attempt DPC wire insertion prior to ECMO cannulation Do NOT attempt DPC wire insertion
Arterial wire technique Use soft wire/MPA exchange technique Insert Amplatz extra-stiff wire directly through arterial sheath
Dilatation technique Multiple sequential dilatation Skipped steps dilatation
Venous cannula size 21-29Fr multistage 21-25Fr multistage
Arterial cannula size 15-19Fr single stage (Biomedicus or EOPA) 15Fr single stage (Biomedicus)

Key Points Summary

  • Excellent teamwork essential: Defined roles for all personnel
  • USS-guided vessel access: CFA 2cm caudal to inguinal ligament
  • DPC wire placement early: In SFA during arterial access (if time allows)
  • Sequential dilatation: 2Fr steps to final size (cardiogenic shock)
  • Venous cannula to SVC: ~55cm at skin for bi-caval drainage
  • Arterial cannula pushed into taper: Ensure secure position
  • Surgical backup available: Low threshold to call cardiac surgery for assistance
  • Initial ECMO flows 3-4 L/min: Titrate to shock markers and LV loading
  • DPC insertion after ECMO established: Within 2-4 hours maximum
  • E-CPR modifications: Rapid technique, skipped steps, no DPC wire initially
  • ACT 200-220 at cannulation: 25-50 iu/kg UFH bolus
  • Antibiotics at cannulation: Teicoplanin/Gentamicin
