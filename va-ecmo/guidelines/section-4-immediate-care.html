Section 4: Immediate Care After Initiating V-A ECMO

Post-cannulation pathway and physiological targets

Note: For comprehensive details on specific aspects of immediate care, see the dedicated topic pages:
  • Initial Management
  • LV Distention Management
  • Harlequin Syndrome
  • Distal Limb Perfusion
  • NIRS Monitoring

Location and Patient Pathway

Depending on location of V-A ECMO instigation, the below tasks may require the patient to be moved through a variety of pathways. The standard pathway is likely to be:

Standard Post-Cannulation Pathway

  1. Location of cannulation:
    • Establish monitoring / relative stabilisation of ventilatory, shock and metabolic parameters
    • Insertion of distal perfusion cannula
    • Baseline TTE or TOE (Level 2) to determine requirements in cath lab
  2. Cardiac Cath labs:
    • Coronary angiogram +/- proceed, LV venting device (if required), DPC (if difficult)
    • This can be delayed by up to 12hrs in specific circumstances (no indication for LV venting device, clear non-coronary pathology, distal perfusion cannulae in situ)
  3. CT scan:
    • Only if complete stability – otherwise move to ICU and plan for CT in first 24-48hrs
  4. A600 (GICU) admission:
    • Followed by transfer to RBH/HH until the service is locally commissioned

Essential Interventions

Monitoring Lines

  • Arterial line insertion into the RIGHT RADIAL OR BRACHIAL artery
    • Critical: Right upper body arterial monitoring to detect Harlequin syndrome
  • Central line insertion – recommend LIJ to leave RIJ for PAC / Vascath
  • Pulmonary artery catheter insertion – once on ICU

Imaging and Diagnostics

  • Echocardiography (TTE or TOE): Level 2 scan, prior to cath lab, specifically including:
    • LV distension (will determine if LV venting device is required when in cath lab)
    • Aortic valve opening
    • LVOT VTi
    • Left and Right ventricular systolic function assessment
    • Cannulae position / evidence of mis-adventure (pericardial tamponade etc)
  • Coronary angiogram +/- proceed – including LVEDP measurement
  • CT scan: Head / Neck (if OHCA) / CTPA / CT Aortogram / CT Abdo+Pelvis

Mechanical Support

  • Distal perfusion cannulae insertion, if not performed at time of ECMO cannulation
  • Left ventricular venting device, if there is evidence of LV distention on echo despite optimisation of ECMO flows and intrinsic inotropy:
    • Intra-aortic balloon pump or
    • Impella CP

Monitoring Systems

  • NIRS monitoring of the lower distal limbs bilaterally (to detect inadequate distal perfusion) and bilaterally on the head for cerebral oximetry. Requires a 4 sensor monitor

Physiological Targets

Ventilation

Maintenance of effective but safe ventilation is required, as standard configuration V-A ECMO requires native gas exchange to continue to prevent Harlequin syndrome.

  • Use BiPAP and start with ECMO rest settings (PInsp 10, PEEP 10, RR 10)
  • Decarboxylation is achieved by titrating ECMO sweep gas flows, rather than ventilator MV
  • In RH failure, lower PEEP levels and higher ventilator MV (to reduce pulmonary circulation PCO₂) may be required to reduce PVR
  • PInsp can be adjusted to achieve Vt 6-8 mls/kg, but keep driving pressure <14
  • Optimal PEEP – includes looking at LV distention as well as standard markers
  • Ventilator FiO₂ titrate to SpO₂ 94-98% / PaO₂ >8 kPa on R ARM SpO₂ probe / art line
  • ECMO fresh gas FiO₂ titrated to PaO₂ 12-15 kPa on post-oxy gas
  • ECMO fresh gas flow titrated to PaCO₂ 4.0-5.5 kPa on systemic arterial line gas

Circulation

Targets are based on a careful balance of:

  • Reversal of organ shock indices
  • Maintaining some native cardiac output (to avoid LV distention/thrombosis)
  • Optimising LV unloading, to encourage native cardiac recovery

LV Unloading Package

LV unloading is not just LV venting to prevent distention – it encompasses a package of physiological requirements to ensure maximal restoration of native cardiac function:

  • Decreased PCWP (pulmonary capillary wedge pressure)
  • Decreased MVO₂ (myocardial oxygen consumption)
  • Improved CBF (coronary blood flow)
  • Myocardial salvage
  • Limit/reverse remodelling

Target Parameters - Gradual Correction Toward:

  • Lactate <2.0
  • BE > -5.0
  • pH >7.35
  • Reducing bilirubin and ALT
  • Cerebral oximetry >55% (although a trend rather than absolute values is favoured)
  • SvO₂ > 60%

All on ECMO post-oxy and R arm blood gases

To Achieve Optimal Circulation:

  • Start low dose inotrope (adrenaline 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min, or milrinone 0.1-0.3 mcg/kg/min) once ECMO flows are established. Titrate to aortic valve opening on echo and arterial line pulsatility of >10 mmHg pulse pressure. If this is not achieved with low dose inotropes, insert an LV venting device
  • Titrate vasoactives to MAP 50-55, to keep afterload minimal but CoPP adequate
  • HR 60-100 bpm, sinus rhythm – utilise antiarrhythmics and digoxin, but avoid beta blockers
  • Arrhythmias are a sign of LV distention / loading – and are an indication for insertion of an LV venting device if not already in situ
  • Minimise V-A ECMO flows (minimum 2 L/min) to reduce afterload and encourage native cardiac output; whilst maintaining appropriate shock indices
  • Regular echocardiography (minimum daily and PRN if LV loading / ejection concerns)
  • Low threshold for LV venting device, if LV distention or reduced aortic valve opening occurs despite optimal vasoactives and ECMO flows
  • Utilise PAC to monitor for increased LV loading (PADP or PCWP >18 mmHg are an indication of LV distention)
  • The absence of electrical activity or pulse pressure in the first few hours of a V-A ECMO run do not necessarily confer poor prognosis

Renal Management

If adequate urine output, fluid balance and metabolic indices can be achieved through native renal function, this should be allowed. However, there should be a low threshold for renal replacement therapy instigation, rather than manipulating vasoactives / fluid balance / ECMO flows to achieve native renal function.

Neurological Management

Awake Patients

Awake patients should be provided with adequate anxiolysis to reduce myocardial work and SVR. This may be achieved with low dose opioids, benzodiazepines or alpha-2 agonists – but may require significant sedation, necessitating invasive ventilation.

Ventilated Patients

If ventilated, patients should be kept RASS -2 to -5 for the 24-48hrs whilst stability is achieved.

  • Vasoactive sparing agents (midazolam and fentanyl) are preferred
  • Low threshold for paralysis infusions during this time

Cerebral Monitoring

Cerebral oximetry should be monitored and values <55-60% either unilaterally or bilaterally should be responded to (see Appendix 4).

Gastrointestinal Management

  • An NG tube should be sited early in the stay to ensure delivery of antiplatelet therapy is achieved
  • Laxatives and a PPI should be prescribed as standard
  • NG feeding should start slowly, once shock indices are correcting
  • Low threshold for providing only trophic enteral feeding and utilising parenteral nutrition for calorific requirement, given a high likelihood of ischaemic / shocked gut
  • Early referral to dieticians is required

Haematological Management

Many patients will require anti-platelet therapy for their cardiac pathology, as well as anticoagulation for the mechanical support.

Prior to Starting Anticoagulation

Prior to starting anticoagulation for the ECMO, there should be:

  • Arrest of any significant peripheral bleeding from line sites; and
  • CT excluding significant intra-cranial or chest/abdominal bleeding (if stable enough for CT)

Standard Anticoagulation

Once confirmed, an unfractionated heparin infusion should be started utilising the ECMO protocol (target anti Xa 0.3-0.5).

Monitoring

  • At least twice daily clotting screens, including fibrinogen, should be sent
  • All patients should have a valid crossmatch sample at all times

Transfusion Thresholds

  • Ensure Hb is kept >80-90 g/L, with low threshold for investigation of occult bleeding if >1 unit of RBC transfusion is required per day
  • Ensure platelets >50 and fibrinogen >1.5 always. Transfuse products as required to achieve and consider circuit change if there is an ongoing consumptive coagulopathy

Management of Significant Bleeding

If significant bleeding occurs:

  1. Perform a TEG for rapid identification of systemic clotting abnormalities and administer products to correct as required
  2. Repeat formal lab tests and target fibrinogen >2.0, platelets >75 and INR <1.3
  3. Stop the heparin infusion and consider reversal with up to ¼ dose protamine
    • CAUTION: High risk of clotting in ECMO circuit
  4. Administer tranexamic acid (TXA) infusion:
    • Prepare 50ml syringe with "neat" TXA solution (5g)
    • Loading dose of 12.5 mg/kg given over 30 minutes
    • Maintenance infusion of 6.5 mg/kg/hour started immediately after loading dose and continuing until syringe empty (approx. 10 hrs) or bleeding ceased for 2hrs
    • Reduce maintenance infusion to 3 mg/kg/hr if continuing beyond 5 grams
    • Maintenance infusion rate to be reduced further if renal or hepatic dysfunction present. Loading dose should remain unchanged
    • Main risk is acute thrombotic events, including circuit thrombosis
  5. Local haemostasis techniques, including interventional radiology and surgical interventions, should also be performed
  6. If despite correction of all of these areas bleeding persists, consider Factor VIIa after discussion with haematology

Infection Management

Temperature Control

Maintain temperature at:

  • 36-36.5°C throughout V-A ECMO run (standard)
  • E-CPR: 35°C for the first 24-48hrs and then 36-36.5°C throughout V-A ECMO run

Utilise the ECMO heater-cooler unit +/- surface cooling devices to achieve this.

Antimicrobials

  • Low threshold for broad spectrum antibiotics

Other Essential Actions

  • Refer patient to local palliative care at the start of the next business day
  • Early referral to local heart failure service and regional advanced heart failure service

Key Points Summary

  • Right radial/brachial arterial line MANDATORY: Detect Harlequin syndrome
  • 4-sensor NIRS monitoring: Bilateral cerebral + bilateral lower limb
  • Early echo assessment: LV distention, AV opening, biventricular function
  • Coronary angiography within 12 hours: Include LVEDP measurement
  • DPC within 2-4 hours: If not inserted at cannulation
  • ECMO rest ventilation: BiPAP PInsp 10, PEEP 10, RR 10
  • Minimize ECMO flows to 2-3 L/min: Reduce afterload, encourage native output
  • MAP 50-55 mmHg: Minimize afterload while maintaining coronary perfusion
  • Low-dose inotropes: Maintain AV opening and pulse pressure >10 mmHg
  • Low threshold for LV venting: If distention despite optimal medical management
  • Anticoagulation after safety checks: Target anti-Xa 0.3-0.5
  • Hb >80-90 g/L, platelets >50, fibrinogen >1.5: Higher thresholds if bleeding
  • Temperature 36-36.5°C: 35°C for first 24-48hrs post E-CPR
  • Early MDT involvement: Palliative care, heart failure service, RBH/HH
