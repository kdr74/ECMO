Section 7: Post-Cardiotomy Patient Specifics

Special considerations for VA-ECMO following cardiac surgery

Key Principle: There is increasing evidence of higher morbidity and mortality with prolonged periods in cardiogenic shock post-cardiotomy, compared with intra-operative or rapid post-operative instigation of V-A ECMO. Therefore, consider an initial plan of V-A ECMO early rather than prolonged attempts at conventional management.

Indications

V-A ECMO implementation indications in post-cardiotomy patients are:

  • Intraoperative failure to wean from cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), because of univentricular or biventricular failure
  • Post-operative delayed refractory cardiogenic shock, cardiac arrest or intractable ventricular arrhythmias
  • Respiratory failure intra- or post-operative may also require ECMO support, although individual circumstances will dictate a V-A, V-V or hybrid mode ECMO configuration

Pre-planned High-Risk Cases

In high risk patients, the requirement for V-A ECMO support post operatively may be pre-planned. This requirement should be communicated between the cardiac surgical, cardiac anaesthesia, ECMO and other MDT members (i.e. ACHD team) pre-operatively, to confirm patient candidacy and the criteria for instigation. This is especially important for the ACHD patients.

Early Recognition and Intervention

Critical Timing: Consider an initial plan of V-A ECMO for patients with worsening cardiogenic shock markers who meet the criteria below - aim to instigate V-A ECMO within 6 hours of leaving theatre if continued worsening.

Intra-operative Criteria

Unable to liberate from CPB due to cardiac failure, despite:

  • >3 optimised vasoactives +/- IABP, OR
  • VIS Score >30

Post-operative Criteria

On >3 optimised vasoactives +/- IABP, OR VIS Score >20

AND

Worsening cardiogenic shock markers (lactate, pH, urine output, PAC variables – CI, CPO, PCWP, PAPi)

Decision Making Process

Decision making for these patients should follow the path of:

  1. Inform Bristol ECMO consultant when in theatre (unable to wean from CPB), or within 2hrs of leaving theatre, for candidacy assessment
  2. MDT decision (cardiac anaesthesia, cardiac surgical and ECMO team) on conventional management optimisation and criteria for definition of conventional management failure
  3. For post-operative patients on CICU:
    • 2 hourly bedside MDT reviews. If worsening, shock call with HH/RBH teams
    • If continued worsening, aim to instigate V-A ECMO within 6hrs of leaving theatre

Instigating V-A ECMO

Cannulation

Intra-operative - Unable to Wean from Bypass

For patients unable to wean from bypass the preferential procedure is to maintain central cannulation and perform a wet circuit change from the existing CPB to the ECMO circuit. Haemostasis must be confirmed prior to considering this procedure.

Post-operative

For post-operative patients, cannulation may be peripheral (femoral or axillary) or central.

Peripheral cannulation may be preferred to reduce the incidence of bleeding and infection, even in the presence of a sternotomy or thoracotomy. Peripheral cannulation should be performed via a percutaneous or hybrid approach (see section 3 – Surgical Assistance).

Anticoagulation

When transferring the patient from CPB to V-A ECMO, a lower anticoagulation level should be targeted (ACT 200-220) to reduce bleeding risk.

  • Dependent on the ACT, often a ¼ to ½ dose of protamine (2.5-5mg protamine per 1000 units UFH delivered) is given just prior to circuit change
  • Ensure ACT >180 prior to circuit change
  • The ECMO circuit should still be heparin primed

Chest Closure

There is debate regarding an open vs closed chest in patients requiring V-A ECMO post cardiotomy. A closed chest limits the bleeding risk, but an open chest allows room for the RV to functionally improve.

In General:

  • Intra-operative or <48hrs post-operative ECMO instigation:
    • Leave the chest open for 24-48hrs
    • Close once shock has cleared and native cardiac recovery has begun to occur
  • >48hrs Post-operative ECMO instigation:
    • If stability can be achieved without re-opening the chest, then leave the chest closed
    • If the chest needs to be reopened (i.e. as part of the CALS protocol for cardiac arrest) – treat as per the intra-operative ECMO instigation

Immediate Care After V-A ECMO Initiation

Location

Patients will be cared for on CICU (C604) for the first 48-72hrs. After this the patient may move to A600 or the Advanced Heart Failure centre (RBH/HH), dependent on their trajectory and commissioning.

Anticoagulation

The circuit should be run at high (>3L/min) flows but without heparinisation for the first 12-48hrs, to reduce the bleeding risk.

  • Patients with ongoing bleeding may have increased time on a heparin free circuit
  • Generally by 48hrs post-operative a heparin infusion, as per ECMO guidelines, will be started
  • Other haematological parameters are the same as for non-cardiotomy patients

LV Venting

LV unloading strategies are as per non-cardiotomy patients. Within this, LV venting strategies are:

Central V-A ECMO:

It is recommended to insert an LA / PV vent at the time of ECMO initiation.

Peripheral V-A ECMO:

An Impella or IABP should be used as an LV vent, with the same indications and insertion protocols as for non-cardiotomy patients.

Important Note: Although high risk for requiring LV venting, not all post-cardiotomy patients require it.

Weaning and Transfer

Modified Timeframes

If native cardiac function recovery is likely to occur from post CPB stunning, it will normally be evident in 48-72hrs.

Therefore the timeframes included in the "Weaning and transfer within the CS network" section (Section 6) should be adjusted for the post-cardiotomy patient to read "After 48-72hrs" rather than "at 5-7 days".

Perfusion Staffing Considerations

Due to the current staffing model, during the time where a post-cardiotomy patient is supported on V-A ECMO in Bristol, a perfusionist is required to be present at the bedside throughout the run when the patient is cared for on CICU.

Dependent on perfusion staffing levels, this is likely to necessitate closing of one of the other perfusion workstreams – with one of the elective cardiac surgical theatres being the most likely.

For cases pre-planned to be high-risk of requiring V-A ECMO, liaison with perfusion, ECMO, cardiac surgical and cardiac anaesthesia teams is vital to see how a run will impact on other services.

Post-Cardiotomy vs Non-Cardiotomy: Key Differences

Aspect Post-Cardiotomy Non-Cardiotomy
Location of care CICU (C604) for 48-72hrs initially GICU (A600) from admission
Cannulation preference Central (wet circuit change from CPB) or peripheral Peripheral (femoral) preferred
Chest closure Often left open 24-48hrs if cannulated <48hrs post-op N/A
Anticoagulation start Delayed 12-48hrs (bleeding risk), ACT 200-220 at cannulation Start once bleeding excluded, ACT 200-220 at cannulation
LV vent (central ECMO) LA/PV vent at time of initiation recommended N/A (peripheral cannulation)

Weaning assessment After 48-72hrs After 5-7 days
Bleeding risk Higher (surgical site, CPB effects) Lower

Key Points Summary

  • Early intervention reduces mortality: Aim for ECMO within 6 hours post-operatively if worsening shock
  • Intra-operative trigger: Unable to wean from CPB with >3 vasoactives or VIS >30
  • Post-operative trigger: >3 vasoactives or VIS >20 with worsening shock markers
  • Pre-planned high-risk cases: MDT discussion including ECMO team before surgery
  • 2-hourly MDT reviews: For post-op patients on CICU at risk of requiring ECMO
  • Central cannulation preferred intra-op: Wet circuit change from CPB to ECMO
  • Peripheral cannulation acceptable post-op: Reduces bleeding and infection risk
  • Lower anticoagulation target: ACT 200-220 (often requires protamine at circuit change)
  • Delayed heparinisation: 12-48hrs to reduce bleeding risk
  • Chest often left open 24-48hrs: If ECMO within 48hrs of surgery
  • LA/PV vent for central cannulation: Insert at time of ECMO initiation
  • CICU care initially: 48-72hrs then reassess location
  • Earlier weaning assessment: 48-72hrs (vs 5-7 days for non-cardiotomy)
  • Recovery usually from CPB stunning: Evident within 48-72hrs if going to occur
  • Perfusionist continuous presence required: On CICU, may impact elective surgery capacity
