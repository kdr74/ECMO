Appendix 4: Cerebral NIRS Protocol

Near-infrared spectroscopy monitoring and management in VA-ECMO

Purpose: Cerebral NIRS (rSO₂) monitoring is essential in VA-ECMO to detect cerebral hypoperfusion, differential perfusion (harlequin syndrome), and guide interventions to optimise cerebral oxygen delivery.

NIRS Monitoring Setup

Equipment Requirements

  • 4-sensor NIRS monitor required for comprehensive monitoring
  • Bilateral cerebral sensors: Applied to forehead (left and right)
  • Bilateral lower limb sensors: Applied to detect distal limb ischaemia

Target Values

Parameter Normal Range Concerning Values
Cerebral rSO₂ (absolute) >60% (ideally >55-60%) <40%
Inter-hemispheric difference <10-20% >20%
Distal limb rSO₂ >50% <50% or >15% difference between limbs
Important: Consider the trend of rSO₂ values in addition to absolute values. A declining trend may be significant even if absolute values remain within "normal" range.

Initial Assessment: One-Sided NIRS Drop

Unilateral Cerebral NIRS Reduction

Indicates: Differential perfusion between hemispheres - cranial blood supply cannot meet demand in a single hemisphere.

Step 1: Check Head Position (Neutral)

Ensure neck is in neutral position, not rotated or compressed.

Step 2: Clinically Assess

  • Check for focal neurological signs (if patient assessable)
  • Review for arrhythmias or abrupt haemodynamic changes

Step 3: Consider Harlequin Syndrome

Especially if native cardiac output is improving:

  • Check R arm arterial PaO₂ and compare to post-oxygenator
  • Assess aortic valve opening frequency on echo
  • See Section 5: Harlequin Syndrome for management

Step 4: Consider Thrombo-embolic Event / ICH

  • Neurological imaging recommended: CT head ± CT angiography
  • Review anticoagulation status
  • Assess for clot in circuit or cannulae

Initial Assessment: Bilateral NIRS Drop

Bilateral Cerebral NIRS Reduction

Indicates: Cranial oxygen supply cannot meet demand in both hemispheres - risk of hypoxic neurological injury.

Step 1: Check Head Position (Neutral)

Ensure neck is in neutral position.

Step 2: Clinically Assess

Obtain R arm arterial, mixed/central venous and ECMO post-oxy blood gases.

Two Main Categories of Bilateral Drop:

Category A: ↑ Oxygen Consumption

Suspect if:

  • Pain, agitation, awake patient
  • Hyperthermia
  • Seizure (including subclinical seizure - consider EEG)

Management:

  • Optimise sedation / analgesia
  • Treat pain / agitation
  • Active temperature control (target 36-36.5°C, or 35°C for first 24-48hrs post E-CPR)
  • Consider EEG if seizure suspected
  • Avoid hyperthermia
Category B: ↓ Oxygen Delivery

Check for causes:

1. Minimize Oxygen Consumption:

See Category A management above.

2. Optimise Oxygen Delivery:
Increase FiO₂:
  • Optimise Hb (target >80-90 g/L)
  • Check ECMO FiO₂ and increase if needed
  • Check ventilator FiO₂
Optimise Cardiac Output:
  • Check ECMO flows - consider increasing
  • Assess for venous access insufficiency
  • Review native cardiac output contribution
Avoid Hyperthermia:
  • Active temperature control
  • Target 36-36.5°C (or 35°C for E-CPR in first 24-48hrs)
3. Optimise Analgesia:

Adequate sedation and analgesia reduce metabolic demand.

4. Consider Paralysis:

If other measures insufficient, consider neuromuscular blockade to reduce O₂ consumption.

Step 3: Consider Specific Causes

↓ PaCO₂ (Powerful Cerebro-vascular Vasoconstrictor)

Mechanism: Excessive ventilation or sweep gas flow causes hypocapnia → cerebral vasoconstriction → reduced cerebral perfusion

Management:

  • Check arterial PaCO₂
  • Reduce ECMO sweep gas flow if low
  • Reduce minute ventilation if excessive
  • Target PaCO₂ 4.0-5.5 kPa
Significant Harlequin Syndrome

Mechanism: If both cerebral hemispheres are supplied predominantly by native cardiac output with poor native lung gas exchange

Check for:

  • R arm PaO₂ significantly lower than post-oxygenator gas
  • Improving native cardiac output (good pulse pressure, aortic valve opening)
  • Poor native lung function

Management: See Section 5: Harlequin Syndrome

Key Principles

Monitoring Best Practice:

  • Apply NIRS sensors at initiation of VA-ECMO
  • Monitor continuously throughout ECMO run
  • Focus on trends as well as absolute values
  • Document NIRS values in daily MDT review
  • Act early on concerning drops or trends

Limitations of NIRS:

  • Values influenced by probe position
  • External light can interfere with readings
  • Skin pigmentation may affect absolute values (trends remain valid)
  • Cannot detect focal ischaemic events not in sensor field
  • Should be used as part of comprehensive neuromonitoring strategy
